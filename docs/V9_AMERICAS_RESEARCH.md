# V9 Americas: research and bounded coverage

Research date: 2026-10-23. Authored original English and Chinese companion text for 66 new entries. Source register and dates describe what was inspected, not a claim of universal historical verification.

## Inventory and release coverage

- Regions: latin-america: 32, north-america: 34.
- Kinds: culture: 17, event: 23, idea: 4, person: 15, place: 4, polity: 3.
- Workstreams: LA1: 5, LA2: 13, LA3: 9, LA4: 8, NA1: 11, NA2: 12, NA3: 18. A multi-workstream record is counted in each applicable row, so those counts are not additive.
- All seven Americas workstreams NA1–NA3 and LA1–LA4 have bounded additions. LA2 supplies thirteen entries spanning early archaeological societies, food and knowledge systems, Inca organization and the transition into colonial rule, including Pachacuti and Guaman Poma focus biographies.
- The main political timeline gains only three explicitly secondary political phases: Palmares, Chile military rule and Brazil military rule. Communities and archaeological cultures are not forced into state categories.

## Evidence method and limits

Focused searches used institutional, university, museum, archival and community sources in English, Spanish, Portuguese and French. Relevant returned page extracts and primary-document passages were inspected, including contemporary documents and original archaeological research where available. Each source carries its actual retrieval mode. “Opened page extract” means the returned text was inspected, not that an entire book or all linked evidence was read. “Indexed excerpt” means only the indexed institutional text or abstract was available; it must not be represented as full-page verification.

The new summaries are original synthesis, with accompanying Chinese text carrying the same qualifications. Date notes distinguish approximate periods, editorial focus windows, actual events and personal lifespans. Locations are representative and no new boundary polygons, verified itineraries or measured agricultural extents are inferred.

Full-page retrieval failed or was incomplete for some Library of Congress collections, Nobel biographies, the Supreme Court Delgamuukw judgment, the British Museum Pachacuti authority record, the Peru National Library Túpac Amaru notice, the Guatemala agreement landing page and the University of Pennsylvania raised-field PDF. The PMC agriculture article returned a bot-check page and was used only at indexed-abstract level. Some community/museum landing pages returned little text. Alternative accessible institutions support principal claims; access limits remain visible rather than being converted into an assertion that every source was read in full.

Two cited pages from the same institution are not automatically two independent research traditions. Entries drawing on one specialist institution or documentary collection preserve that narrower base. Current legal texts are used only as historical documents; the narratives do not purport to give legal advice or reconstruct every modern land claim.

## Conflicts identified and qualified

1. The NPS Zitkála-Šá biography includes contradictory death years (1928, 1936 and 1938 in different contexts). The entry adopts 1876–1938 from New-York Historical and the National Women’s History Museum; the erroneous NPS sentence is excluded.
2. The NWHM Wilma Mankiller biography calls 1985 an election. The U.S. Mint dates election to 1987. The new entry distinguishes accession in 1985 from election in 1987.
3. Pachacuti reign endpoints differ among institutional chronologies (1471/ca.1473). The record labels a conventional reign focus, not a lifespan.
4. Guaman Poma birth/death chronologies differ; the entry uses a documented writing focus c.1585–1615 and the dated completion letter.
5. Bartolina Sisa early-life dates vary; the entry uses 1781–1782 leadership/execution focus instead of selecting a disputed birth year.
6. Chavín’s UNESCO short description and significance statement give different terminal ranges. The selected c.1500–500 BCE focus is qualified in the date note.
7. Kuélap’s source is a State Party tentative-list submission, not proof that UNESCO has independently endorsed every claim about a unified Chachapoya state or an extinct people. The narrative does not adopt those claims.
8. New Laws of 1542 are not treated as instant enforcement or the universal end of Indigenous slavery. An original research article is read alongside the primary legal text.
9. Horse evidence revises older dispersal stories based only on colonial observation. The Comanche entry distinguishes the selected regional phase from the earliest integration of horses into Indigenous societies.
10. The dated 1982 constitutional event, 1996 Guatemala agreement, 1943 exclusion repeal and 1988 redress are milestones; no entry treats a document as proof that social inequality or implementation disputes immediately ended.

## Remaining scope

These additions develop connected learning paths, not every country or local history. North American regional coverage still needs additional California, Plateau, Southeast and Arctic community histories. Caribbean and Central American histories need more local trajectories. Amazonian, Mapuche, Guaraní and Afro-descendant histories remain underrepresented. Modern Peru, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Uruguay and other national sequences require dedicated batches. No annual premodern border coverage is claimed. Those limits do not cancel the work completed across the seven declared workstreams.
