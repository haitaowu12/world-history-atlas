# V12 United States foundations: research and editorial ledger

Checked **2026-10-24**. Data: `dist/data/expansion-us-foundations-v12.json`.

## Contribution

Eight new bilingual records add **four biographies, four connected local/institutional histories and 17 biography milestones**. All use existing workstream `NA3`, release 6, edition `v12`. No political-overview bars, routes or territorial polygons were added.

| Biography | Connected history | Relationship to existing material |
| --- | --- | --- |
| George Washington | Valley Forge supply systems and camp households | Adds the work of sustaining a military institution alongside existing Revolution, Constitution and Virginia slavery records. |
| Abraham Lincoln | Penn School on Saint Helena Island | Connects emancipation policy to a particular school's wartime emergence. It does not claim Lincoln founded the school or that one site represents all freedpeople's education. |
| Martin Luther King Jr. | Memphis sanitation strike | Extends the existing Montgomery/civil-rights sequence into workplace safety, pay and union recognition, retaining workers' and local organizers' agency. |
| Jane Addams | Hull-House immigrant neighborhood | Connects care, education, inquiry, reform and peace activism to a Chicago institution and its wider team. |

The new school record uses ID **`penn-school-freedom-education`**, replacing the initial planning label `freedmen-schools-reconstruction` before integration. Its 1862–1865 focus is supported by the specific Penn section of the inspected source. It neither assigns an unsupported generic Bureau history to Penn's coordinates nor dates the entire educational movement by one institution.

Existing IDs and titles were reviewed across the V11 cumulative inventory, including the Revolution, Declaration, Constitution, Civil War, Montgomery boycott, civil-rights movement, Great Migration, Douglass, Tubman and Wells. The four context entries develop different subjects rather than duplicating those overview records. Every new record links to its pair and at least one earlier entry.

## How source inspection was performed

Page navigation, bibliography links and embedded media were not automatically treated as read. No historical speech passage, photograph, manuscript image or source paragraph was imported into the app.

## Context dates and geography

- **Valley Forge, 1777–1778:** W2 dates the camp to 19 December 1777–19 June 1778. Its point represents the encampment region. Hospitals, farms, commandeered property and supply links were distributed beyond it. Disease and supply reform are historical explanations, not present medical guidance or a new quantitative effectiveness claim.
- **Penn School, 1862–1865:** P1 distinguishes Army control of the island in 1861, the Old Brick Church's construction in 1855 and school opening in 1862. The selected focus ends at the described wartime expansion by 1865, not at school closure. The representative Saint Helena Island point does not map every building. Later Penn Center work is prose aftermath, not an event assigned to 1865.
- **Memphis, 1968:** K2 distinguishes 1 February deaths, 12 February strike, 16 April agreement and later enforcement pressure. The point represents a citywide municipal labor dispute. Local workers and organizers precede King; his death is not the campaign's endpoint.
- **Hull-House, 1889–1915:** Focus from founding to Addams's widened peace organizing, not institutional lifespan. Services developed across several facilities and years; the founding year is not assigned to every program. The map point identifies the settlement locality, not all immigrant Chicago.

## Corrections and omissions during review

1. **Washington and republican exceptionalism:** W1 incorrectly gives Virginia's ratification margin as one vote and moves from unanimous electors to a unanimous popular voice. Neither claim appears in the data. Its sweeping comparative claims about kingship, uniquely virtuous administration and global imitation are omitted. The narrative includes enslaved labor, limits of the will, civilian costs and expansion's pressure on Indigenous land/authority.
2. **Valley Forge figures:** W2 contains differing hut and camp-population figures across sections. No precise hut/population total is adopted. Its broader description of gender, coercion, class and service motives is retained without pretending that everyone shared one ideal of liberty.
3. **Lincoln proclamation scope:** L1's suggestion that all enslaved people in all Confederate states were freed is qualified using L2's explicit territorial exceptions and dependence on military power. January 1863 is not nationwide abolition, and enslaved people's own actions are retained. L1's cemetery name and compressed 1836 qualification/Springfield chronology are not used for milestones; no relocation year is invented.
4. **War's end:** The narrative does not repeat a claim that Lee's Virginia surrender immediately ended all Civil War fighting. Lincoln's death, emancipation, later nationwide abolition and Reconstruction are distinct histories. Schooling is not represented as a gift of Lincoln or proof of completed equal citizenship.
5. **King as sole actor:** K1's commemorative language and large activity totals are not adopted. K2 restores T. O. Jones, unions, ministers, students, city conflict and Coretta Scott King's later march. The March violence and police attacks are retained; the agreement is distinguished from guaranteed implementation.
6. **Addams and settlement-house priority:** A2 incorrectly calls Hull-House the first US settlement house. The data excludes that claim and treats it as part of a broader movement. No child-labor-committee foundation year or extended conference presidency is copied from A2. The 1931 hospital location is preserved precisely because award location and personal presence differ.
7. **Programs versus outcomes:** Childcare, classes, educational growth and service descriptions are not claimed as measured equal access, literacy gains or permanent poverty reduction. Residents and workers remain historical agents with their own interests; the atlas does not establish that they all shared the reformers' views.
