# V12 North American regions: knowledge, labour and artistic lives

Checked **24 October 2026**. Data: `dist/data/expansion-north-america-regions-v12.json`.

## Scope and integration

This contribution adds **eight bilingual entries: four developed biographies and four connected histories**, with **20 dated biographical milestones**. It covers the upper Missouri and northern Rockies, Tuskegee in Alabama, Kinngait on southern Baffin Island, and southern Alberta. Each biography contains 239–253 English words, with Chinese narratives preserving the same qualifications. Context histories contain 181–203 English words. These are selected local sequences, not a survey of all U.S. or Canadian history.

The existing 80 North American records were checked for IDs and topics before drafting. No entry covered Sacagawea, George Washington Carver, Kenojuak Ashevak, John Ware, Fort Mandan, Tuskegee extension or Kinngait printmaking. The new pairs complement, rather than duplicate, existing anchors:

| New pair | Existing context / distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Sacagawea / Fort Mandan and Indigenous knowledge | `louisiana-purchase` supplies the earlier colonial-claim context; this addition develops interpretation, village networks and a woman's selected expedition-period life. It is not another land-purchase or solitary-explorer story. |
| Carver / Tuskegee's demonstration wagon | Slavery and the Great Migration are existing anchors; the new material concerns education, research and a specific teaching mechanism, without treating agricultural advice as the full history of Black rural life. |
| Kenojuak / Kinngait cooperative printmaking | `inuit-thule-mobility` is an earlier archaeological/social history; `nunavut-creation` concerns a later territorial institution. Modern art-making is not dated by either of those historical windows. |
| Ware / southern Alberta ranch work | Métis bison-hunt governance and the CPR last spike already exist. Black cattle work, paid employment, corporate ranching and a family household are distinct subjects. |

## Research access and method

Thirteen distinct supporting URLs appear in the data. Search listings, failed routes, anti-bot pages and a certificate-blocked archive were not accepted as inspected article evidence. Institutional pages can reproduce earlier scholarship; different URLs do not automatically provide independent corroboration. The MHA Board's authorship is distinct from the conventional NPS biography even though both texts are hosted by NPS.

Geographic coordinates are rounded representative editorial points. Sources establish localities and the meaning of a pin, not surveyed coordinates. Camps, meetings, buildings, property boundaries and routes are not reconstructed from this material. A degree or artwork destination does not imply a documented personal itinerary.

## Sacagawea and the upper Missouri

**Records:** `person-sacagawea`, `fort-mandan-indigenous-knowledge`.

**Inspected sources and captures:**

- [NPS: Sacagawea](https://www.nps.gov/people/sacagawea.htm), Supports residence among Mandan/Hidatsa communities, April 1805 departure with Charbonneau and their infant, food/clothing knowledge, Shoshone horse negotiations and 1806 return. Its circa-1788 birth, conventional Lemhi Shoshone origin/captivity account and circa-1812 death are not silently promoted to a settled full life.
- [NPS: Hiring of Charbonneau and Sacagawea](https://www.nps.gov/places/hiring-of-charbonneau-and-sacagawea.htm), Supports the prominent trading villages near the Knife River confluence, November 1804 wintering and hiring, Mandan advice about horses, and a multi-person English/French/Hidatsa/Shoshone interpretation chain. Exact maternal age is not adopted.
- [MHA Nation Sacagawea Project Board: Mandan, Hidatsa, and Arikara Histories of Sacagawea](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/mandan-hidatsa-and-arikara-histories-of-sacagawea.htm), The tribal-authored summary explains Hidatsa/Crow family history, adoption-based kinship, food/landmark knowledge and criticism of how the journals recorded identity. The summary also asserts a later death and genealogical evidence. Those claims remain attributed; the complete 2021 book, original journal record, cited interviews and DNA material were not independently examined.
- [NPS: Birth of Pomp](https://www.nps.gov/places/birth-of-pomp.htm), Supports the 11 February 1805 birth of Jean Baptiste in the Knife River village context and April departure. Imagined emotions, exact age and later child-care chronology are not adopted.

**Milestones:**

| Year | Event / source support | Pin meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1804 | Recruitment near the villages (Hiring article) | Upper Missouri / Fort Mandan wintering locality near present Washburn; not the surveyed original fort. |
| 1805 | Birth of Jean Baptiste on 11 February (Birth article) | Knife River–Missouri village area; no exact earth lodge. |
| 1805 | April departure (NPS biography and Birth article) | Expedition wintering context; not a reconstructed daily route. |
| 1805 | Assistance securing Shoshone horses (NPS biography; interpretation and kinship context from MHA) | Broad northern Rockies / present Montana–Idaho meeting region; not a council-site claim. |
| 1806 | Return to Mandan/Hidatsa villages (NPS biography) | Village region, rather than St. Louis or a later death site. |

**Interpretive decisions:** The biography is explicitly **1804–1806 expedition-period focus**, not lifespan. It does not choose between incompatible origins/death traditions, invent a precise birth, or use adoption language as a modern biological-family chart. Conventional NPS and MHA interpretations appear as attributed accounts. The narrative avoids claiming that she alone guided the entire journey or that knowledge exchange constituted Indigenous consent to U.S. territorial claims.

The companion explains the mechanism of interpretation and existing village knowledge. It does not portray Fort Mandan as the origin of organized life on an empty landscape. No horse-acquisition point becomes territorial geometry or an inferred route leg.

## George Washington Carver and Tuskegee teaching

**Records:** `person-george-washington-carver`, `tuskegee-mobile-agricultural-education`.

**Inspected sources and captures:**

- [NPS: George Washington Carver](https://www.nps.gov/people/george-washington-carver.htm), Supports enslavement in Missouri, educational exclusion and mobility, Kansas farming, 1890 Simpson study, 1891 Iowa State entry, the 1896 master's degree, Tuskegee teaching, crop rotation and 1943 death. The header's exact 12 July 1864 birth is not adopted; neither are lists of hundreds of product applications treated as verified inventions.
- [NPS: History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/gwca/learn/historyculture/index.htm), Includes a transcription of Carver's retrospective autobiographical letter. NPS explicitly warns that early recollections are vague and the document is not a definitive life source. It supports his described interests, school exclusion, movement and support networks, but not an exact birth reconstructed from his remembered age at the war's close.
- [Tuskegee University: The Legacy of Dr. George Washington Carver](https://www.tuskegee.edu/legacy/george-washington-carver.html), The visible institutional text supports the intended audience of poor farmers, cotton-exhausted land, alternatives including soybeans/peanuts, accessible free brochures, soil/water analysis, livestock/food-preservation teaching, and the 1906 demonstration wagon. The accordion chronology, complete bulletins, embedded media and outside archive were not reviewed.

**Milestones:** 1890 art/piano study at Simpson in Indianola; 1891 entry to Iowa State in Ames; 1896 master's degree in Ames; 1906 demonstration-wagon design associated with Tuskegee; 5 January 1943 death in Tuskegee. The first three and death are supported by the NPS person biography; 1906 comes from Tuskegee's institutional text. Institutional-city pins are not classroom, wagon-route or gravesite coordinates.

**Scope and omissions:** The selected biography covers **1890–1943**, not a falsely exact 1864–1943 lifespan. The companion uses **1906** to date the wagon design, while its date note distinguishes the broader educational practices from that single year. Tuskegee spells the wagon `Jessup` on the inspected page; the narrative calls it a demonstration wagon rather than resolving spelling against an unread source. No inventor-of-peanut-butter myth, quantified farmer-income improvement, sole-authorship claim for extension services, or invented staff roster is included. The record explains design and educational intention, not a measured outcome.

## Kenojuak Ashevak and Kinngait printmaking

**Records:** `person-kenojuak-ashevak`, `kinngait-cooperative-printmaking`.

**Inspected sources and captures:**

- [The Canadian Encyclopedia: Kenojuak Ashevak](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/kenojuak-ashevak), Supports 1927–2013, land-based childhood, 1952–1955 treatment in Quebec City, graphic practice, 1960 `The Enchanted Owl`, a 1970s stamp, the 2004 Appleby College stained glass and later work. Its claim about a first woman in the print shop and exact first-print/catalogue stages is not adopted.
- [Inuit Art Foundation: Kenojuak Ashevak](https://www.inuitartfoundation.org/profiles/artist/Kenojuak-Ashevak), Supports grandmother's teaching, creative work with Johnniebo, Kinngait arts programme, 1960 owl, **1970** stamp and 1927/2013 dates. The captured visible biography ends with a truncated awards paragraph; unavailable continuation and embedded video are not claimed inspected.
- [Dorset Fine Arts: institutional overview](https://www.dorsetfinearts.com/), canonical homepage text captured in (after the candidate artist route had displayed the same institutional overview). Supports the annual graphics collection since 1959, Kinngait cooperative context and 1978 Toronto wholesale marketing division. This generic institutional text is not treated as an artist-specific profile.
- [Dorset Fine Arts: Printmaking in Kinngait](https://www.dorsetfinearts.com/printmaking), Supports annual catalogued editions from 1959, stonecut drawing/tracing/cutting/inking/hand-pulling stages, and early-1970s lithography. The cigarette-package origin anecdote and longest-running-studio superlative are not adopted.

**Milestones:** 1952 beginning of the documented 1952–1955 hospital period (Encyclopedia; representative Quebec City); 1960 `The Enchanted Owl` (both biographies; Kinngait work context); 1970 stamp (IAF's explicit date; pin remains the artist's community and does not assert travel to Ottawa); 2004 window (Encyclopedia; Oakville artwork destination, not proof of a visit); 2013 death (both profiles; Kinngait).

**Scope and production credit:** The biography uses **1927–2013**, supported by both sources, while omitting an inferred exact camp coordinate. Ikirasaq/Ikerrasak are source transliterations. The first-print claim can conflate a drawing date, print production and catalogued release, so no exact 1958/1959 first-print milestone is made. The companion's **1959–1978** span links annual editions to the later marketing division and includes changing methods. It does not date the origin of Inuit creativity or imply an end to the cooperative.

The process account distinguishes drawing, stonecutting and hand-pulling without inventing the personnel for every impression. Design credit is not proof that the designer personally performed all production stages. A later stamp reproduction is distinct from the original print.

## John Ware and southern Alberta ranch work

**Records:** `person-john-ware`, `southern-alberta-black-ranching`.

**Inspected sources and captures:**

- [The Canadian Encyclopedia: John Ware](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/john-ware), The article, edited with material from Cheryl Foggo, explicitly discusses sparse early records and later legends. Supports circa 1845–1850 birth uncertainty, 1882 cattle drive, 1885 four-nines brand, 1892 marriage, 1902 move and 1905 death. Its 1887 own-ranch date differs from Parks Canada's 1888; neither is silently selected. The retrospective extraordinary-feat stories are not repeated as facts.
- [Parks Canada: John Ware National Historic Person](https://parks.canada.ca/culture/designation/personnage-person/john-ware), Plaque/backgrounder supports enslavement with unclear early details, 1882 Bar U drive, employment with corporate ranches, marriage/family, 1902 Red Deer River move and 1905 deaths. It explicitly identifies Iyarhe Nakoda, Siksikaitsitapi and Ktunaxa traditional territories, preventing empty-frontier framing.

**Milestones:** 1882 drive to the Bar U operation (both; destination district near Longview); 1885 brand registration (Encyclopedia; representative Sheep Creek ranching context, not a registration-office pin); 1892 marriage (both; household locality near Millarville); 1902 Red Deer River relocation (both; representative Duchess district); 1905 riding-accident death (both; not a surveyed accident site).

**Scope and conflicts:** Both records use **1882–1905 Alberta-career focus**, not lifespan. Birthplace is not assigned, and the uncertain birth range is explained separately. The 1887/1888 first-ranch discrepancy remains explicit; general prose and other supported milestones avoid fabricated certainty. Household and corporate labour are connected without inventing specific tasks for Mildred or each child. The sources' admiration does not establish that Ware's reputation overcame systemic racism or that legal land-title questions have been resolved. Exact cause(s) of Mildred's illness are not included.
