# Gupta and Ayutthaya follow-up · 27 October 2026

This release adds dated evidence, seven source-derived South Asian regional core instances, and a single unfilled 1580 Toungoo sphere reference. It does not claim to have resolved annual borders or the complete extent of either imperial Gupta India or Toungoo occupation. `GUPTA_AYUTTHAYA_BASELINE.json` pins the 650 earlier map instances and every preceding layer's bytes.

## Imperial and later Gupta evidence

- Harvard's [South Asia timeline](https://timelinesouthasianhistory.hsites.harvard.edu/gupta-v%C4%81k%C4%81%E1%B9%ADaka-age-c-300-%E2%80%93-550-ce) lists Chandragupta I c.319–350; alternative chronologies differ. The [Archaeological Survey of India inscription corpus](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.108395/2015.108395.Corpus-Inscriptionum-Indicarum-Vol3-inscriptions-Of-The-Early-Gupta-Kings_djvu.txt), introduction pp.2–8, discusses later Purana traditions associated with Prayaga, Saketa and Magadha. Connecting them to a specific reign is an interpretation, not a contemporary border survey. The [British Museum coin](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1910-0403-3) depicts and names Chandragupta I and Kumaradevi but is attributed to Samudragupta and dated c.330–376. A representative Prayaga point provides early context; no unsupported 320–349 imperial polygon is inferred.
- The ASI corpus no.43 pp.352–354 converts the Eran inscription's year 191 to 510–511. It describes a battle involving Bhanugupta but says the inscription does not identify a reigning king and he need not have been a sovereign. The Eran point records the vicinity, not its jurisdiction.
- The Damodarpur charter has Gupta year 224 and a damaged ruler name. ASI corpus no.47 pp.360–364 converts it to **542–543**, while [Siddham's metadata](https://siddham.network/inscription/in00056/?section=metadata) gives **543–545** and leaves the ruler unknown. The combined 542–545 date interval is a union of conversion ranges, not continuous activity; attribution to Vishnugupta remains tentative. The [Siddham object record](https://siddham.network/object/ob00051/) gives the representative findspot (88.711069 E, 25.505149 N), correcting the less precise prior test witness (88.85 E, 25.32 N). Neither inscription establishes a territorial frontier or continuity of direct rule across intervening years.
- The [550](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:South_Asia_historical_AD550_EN.svg) and [590](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:South_Asia_historical_AD590_EN.svg) Woudloper maps (CC BY-SA 4.0) distinguish a later Gupta court area from the Maukhari and nearby courts. These are not automatically the same as the imperial Gupta state; their relationship and sequence are debated. [Avantika Lal's Maukhari account](https://www.worldhistory.org/Maukhari_Dynasty/) describes independence around 554 and a possible Maukhari occupation of Magadha, which does not simply agree with the map's 590 later Gupta placement. The map is displayed as one approximate interpretation; its display window is not a claim of unchanging rule. The source artist lists historical books, but those books were not independently inspected for the depicted geometry.
- The 550 map supplies Later Gupta, Maukhari and Chalukya source-colored cores, shown as **550–589** context; the 590 map supplies those and Pushyabhuti, shown as **590–599** context. No map colored as an imperial Gupta frontier is extrapolated into 501–549. These intervals are explicit editorial viewing windows, not reconstructed annual boundaries. Maukhari before c.554 is described as a court area, not asserted independence. Existing Chalukya reference resumes at 600.

## Ayutthaya and Toungoo

- [Pamaree Surakiat, Journal of the Siam Society 93 (2005), pp.91–92 and 95–97](https://thesiamsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2005/03/JSS_093_0c_PamareeSurakiat_ThaiBurmeseWarfareDuring16thCentu.pdf), distinguishes direct administration near Pegu from tributary Ayutthaya and Lan Xang. It describes Bayinnaung's reorganization and garrisoning of Ye, Tavoy, Tenasserim and Mergui following the 1564 victory, and Ayutthaya's 1593 Tavoy/Tenasserim operations and gradual recovery of the wider region. New 1564 and 1593 points mark Tavoy vicinity, not continuous coastal control or a perimeter. Existing 1569–1580 vassal, 1581–1583 disputed transition and 1584–1599 restoration labels are retained; see `LATER_MAP_GAPS_REVIEW.md` for the 1581/1584 source disagreement.
- [Soewinhan's 1580 map](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_Taungoo_Empire_(1580).png), CC BY-SA 3.0, shades one broad sphere and explicitly warns that its borders were not demarcated. It does not distinguish direct occupation from subordinate courts; its southern extent is cropped. We show its outer contour as an **unfilled dashed comparison**, with the crop left open and no uniform occupation fill. The 1580 outline is made available as a fixed comparison during 1569–1580; it is not asserted as an actual perimeter for those earlier years. The cited historical study supports different kinds of political relationship, not this outline. The source map lists works by Lieberman and others; those cited books were not independently inspected to validate geometry.
- Retained source PNG SHA-256 `de7ab082e0dcce5f97704ae8ebacb5566e6c336b62c2a44a49ab458af3001b5b`. The largest green region was color-thresholded, four-pixel closed and traced; no new historical data were invented. A two-source-pixel simplification is far below map/source uncertainty. The source is fitted using 14 city markers against the pinned Natural Earth populated-place reference: median residual about 26 km, maximum about 80 km, and leave-one-out median about 36 km, maximum about 108 km. These are registration diagnostics, not accuracy estimates for historical control. The southern image-crop seam is never rendered. The stored polygon exists for the atlas's polygon data schema; only its open linework is drawn, without fill.

## Remaining gap and source limits

A reliable, reusable early Gupta border before 350, a continuous imperial Gupta border for 501–549, and a dated perimeter of direct Toungoo occupation around Ayutthaya remain unestablished here. Later maps should be compared with identifiable scholarly cartography or primary evidence before replacing these references. The visual impression of political shading should not exceed what each source actually supports.
