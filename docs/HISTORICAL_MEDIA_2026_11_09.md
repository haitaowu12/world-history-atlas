# Historical media intake — 9 November 2026

## Delivered scope

This intake adds **16 locally served images**: ten images of historical people, four photographs of surviving objects, and two manuscript details. The image files total **2,811,969 bytes**. The owning manifest is [`dist/data/history-media.json`](../dist/data/history-media.json), and the distributable credit notice is [`dist/assets/history-media/NOTICE.md`](../dist/assets/history-media/NOTICE.md).

The selection supports existing biographies and the V16 stories; it is not complete portrait coverage of the People directory. It includes photographs, ancient royal representation, later artistic depictions and material evidence. Each entry identifies which type it is. No generated faces or reconstructed historical scenes have been added.

## Evidence and image identity

Object/file pages were opened on 9 November 2026, and their actual metadata and rights sections were inspected. The six Met images were also checked through the museum's Open Access API (`isPublicDomain: true`). All 16 downloaded files were decoded, compared with their stated subjects in a visual contact sheet, and checked for dimensions, byte counts and SHA-256 hashes. The manifest retains the exact source page, original image URL and downloaded image URL; a supplied thumbnail can differ from the original resolution.

## Decisions made during review

- **Murasaki:** the frequently reused `File:Murasaki Shikibu.jpg` has inconsistent metadata: a seventeenth-century date alongside an artist who lived 1781–1878 and an 1868 book source. It was not used. Hiroshige's c. 1855 triptych provides a documented alternative and remains labelled a later depiction.
- **Mansa Musa:** the file simply named `Mansa Musa.jpg` describes a twentieth-century reproduction. This intake uses the BnF/Gallica-derived detail of the medieval manuscript instead.
- **Su Shi:** the NPM page contains descriptions that sometimes compress poem composition and scroll writing into one date. Its more specific Chinese and English accounts distinguish the 1082 poems from later transcription. The caption follows that distinction. The page's default gallery image showed a later title/collection section; a different inspected gallery image showing the poem was selected.
- **Objects:** artifact dates, current museum accession dates, photographic dates and historical narrative dates remain distinct. No image supplies territorial boundaries or a precise historical location.
- **Coverage:** other biographies remain without images until a suitable, identified, reusable image is located. Availability is not an assessment of a person's historical importance.

## Rights and transformations

The Met's [image policy](https://www.metmuseum.org/policies/image-resources) identifies its Open Access public-domain images as CC0. This intake uses the museum's supplied `web-large` files and retains accession numbers and credit lines.

The two NPM object pages expressly distinguish CC0 lower/presentation images from CC BY 4.0 higher-resolution images. The included files are the public 600-pixel gallery presentations, downloaded without visual changes. Their source color targets and scales remain visible. The gallery photographs already show details of longer scrolls; the atlas did not crop them.

The Kartini file is **CC BY-SA 3.0**, not CC0 or atlas-original CC BY. Its required museum credit and license URL are retained in the manifest and the bundled notice. Its supplied 534 × 700 bytes are unchanged. The Liliʻuokalani file includes a pre-existing restoration; James J. Williams and Adam Cuerden are both credited.

Other Commons files retain their specific public-domain bases. Commons supplied the reduced-size files where available. There are no local crops, colorization, generative edits or face reconstructions. A thumbnail download is recorded separately from its original image URL.

The final Murasaki file is Wikimedia's supplied **500 × 251-pixel thumbnail**, **234,514 bytes**, of the same Hiroshige triptych. It replaces the earlier 960-pixel presentation file; the subject, full three-panel composition, source, date, captions and later-depiction qualification are unchanged. The final thumbnail was decoded and visually inspected, and its dimensions, byte count and SHA-256 hash match the manifest. No local crop or visual edit was applied.

Direct attempts to open the Art Institute and Library of Congress detail pages in the web reader returned access errors. No fresh inspection of those pages is claimed; their collection attributions here come from the inspected Commons file records. This distinction does not apply to the six directly inspected Met records or the two NPM records.

## Verification and integration

All images decode successfully and their dimensions, file sizes and SHA-256 hashes match the manifest. Every item has nonempty English and Chinese alt text, captions, interpretation notes and transformation notes, plus linked source and rights information. New narrative IDs are resolved by the V16 integration; the ten portrait IDs already belong to existing biographies.

The application should use the local `file` path, preserve aspect ratio, display the caption and image-specific rights/source, and expose the interpretation note for later depictions and manuscript details. Gallery images should not silently crop long compositions into apparent headshots. A missing image should leave the sourced narrative usable. Product rendering and browser acceptance are owned by the integration change, not established by these file checks alone.
