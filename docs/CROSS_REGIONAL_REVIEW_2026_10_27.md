The entries use representative points; they do not add a reconstructed migration line, political border or occupation area. ## Cold War and Vietnam

- `cold-war-world-history`: **1945–1991 is an editorial overview**, not an agreed universal start/end. The [State Department early Cold War account](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/foreword) and [1989 account](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1989-1992/fall-of-communism) are explicitly U.S.-foreign-policy sources and describe different regional transitions. The [1991 dissolution account](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1989-1992/collapse-soviet-union) was also directly inspected and retains the same U.S. framing. [Jürgen Dinkel's Wilson Center essay](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/the-non-aligned-movement-and-the-north-south-conflict) contributes a scholarly interpretation of decolonization, nonalignment and North–South relations; its cited underlying books/archives were not separately inspected. The previously reviewed `soviet-union-dissolution` entry retains institutional 1991 sources.
- `nonaligned-belgrade-1961`: Belgrade 1961 is distinct from the existing Bandung 1955 entry. Nonalignment did not imply identical policies or a complete lack of ties to large powers. Berlin and Belgrade points are named representative contexts, not boundaries of ideological blocs.
- `vietnam-war-1954-1975`, `tet-offensive-1968` and `paris-vietnam-agreement-1973`: selected event chronology was checked in the [Naval History and Heritage Command chronology](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/vietnam-war/chronology.html): 1954 Geneva, expanded U.S. operations in 1965, Tết in early 1968, Paris agreement on 27 January 1973 and fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975. The 1954 military demarcation is provisional; the 1973 agreement did not end the fighting. The existing `vietnam-formal-reunification` entry separately dates formal unification to 1976; the [UN authority record](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/3979563?ln=en) was directly inspected for the 2 July 1976 union. The Navy account is U.S.-service-centered; [Harvard's 2025 conference report](https://rajawali.hks.harvard.edu/articles/fifty-years-on-new-perspectives-on-the-vietnam-wars-day-one/) summarizes research on North and South Vietnamese politics and civilians but is not the papers themselves. Existing `person-nguyen-thi-binh` supplies a Vietnamese museum account of her diplomatic role. Laos and Cambodia do not inherit Vietnam's dates automatically.
- **1954 map label correction:** the source codes two Vietnamese units beginning 1 May 1954. The directly inspected [20 July Geneva agreement](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v16/d1035), Articles 1–2 and its entry-into-force clause, instead specifies a provisional military demarcation and a regrouping period, effective 22 July. The 1954 year-end labels and source notes identify a provisional reference; the source start date is not presented as the date of the agreement or proof of two permanent sovereign borders. Source geometry remains unchanged.
- **1975 map label correction:** the inherited CShapes source merges the displayed Vietnamese geometry into its code 816 unit from 1 May 1975 and stops its South Vietnam code 817 on 30 April. Its single area does not separately represent the Republic of South Viet-Nam during the interim. The year-end 1975 label is therefore `Vietnam · 1975 transition reference`, with a bilingual note citing the directly inspected UN 2 July 1976 union record. The source geometry and source dates are unchanged; the label does not claim to reconstruct interim administration or wartime control. Annual 1974 and 1976 views retain their appropriate source names.
- `cuban-missile-crisis-1962`: the [retired U.S. State Department chronology](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/cuban-missile-crisis) distinguishes the mid-October discovery, 22 October quarantine announcement, 28 October removal announcement and later Turkey-missile understanding. This is U.S.-diplomatic evidence; independent Cuban civilian, Cuban government and Soviet archival perspectives remain needed. Havana represents context, not a missile-site coordinate.

## Atlantic and Indigenous histories

- `columbian-exchange-1492-1800`: the [University System of New Hampshire synthesis](https://sites.usnh.edu/hoslac/columbian-exchange-1492-1800/) supplies selected crop, animal and pathogen examples in multiple directions. It is a teaching synthesis; its mortality and disease-origin figures are not imported. The [Smithsonian Taíno exhibition announcement](https://www.si.edu/newsdesk/releases/ta-no-perseverance-subject-upcoming-new-york-city-exhibition-national-museum-american-india) supports survival and living descendants, not an exact count for every community. The 1492–1800 focus is not a claim of first-ever transatlantic contact or the end of Indigenous peoples or exchange.
- `transatlantic-slave-trade-1514-1866`: [David Eltis's SlaveVoyages methods essay](https://www.slavevoyages.org/blog/the-transatlantic-slave-trade-database/166) distinguishes surviving voyage records, variable coverage and estimated figures. [UNESCO's Ali Moussa essay](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/slave-trade-and-slavery-founding-tragedy-our-modern-world) describes resistance and enduring consequences; its broad Africa-wide estimates cannot be silently equated with transatlantic estimates. No total is copied. The database essay discusses **1514–1866**, while the already published schematic Atlantic journey uses a wider **1501–1866** editorial period. Neither range is a universal start/end for slavery. The existing route's regional links are not individual voyages or birthplaces.
- `person-mary-prince`: the [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/the-life-story-of-mary-prince/) gives 1788 as the birth year and supplies the 1828 London arrival and 1831 publication. The atlas treats 1788 as approximate; that qualification is ours, not stated by the museum. The [1831 narrative transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17851/17851-h/17851-h.htm) was inspected at the title page, editor's preface and opening; Prince names Brackish Pond but the inspected opening does not give a birth year. The [National Museum of Bermuda interpretation](https://nmb.bm/history/mary-prince-and-her-bermudian-identity/) discusses the mediation of narrated testimony through transcription and editing. The 1833 endpoint marks the selected documentary trace, not a confirmed death date; each milestone names its representative location.

## African and Pacific migrations

- `bantu-language-early-dispersal`: [Koile et al. (2022)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2112853119), abstract and selected results/discussion inspected, compares several linguistic phylogeographic models. Its roughly 4,420 BP estimate is a modeled narrow-Bantu/Mbam–Bubi split, not the crossing date; its combined model gives an internal split/crossing around 3,890 BP and an East Bantu divergence around 3,150 BP, with intervals and differing definitions. The authors identify scant archaeological evidence for early interior movements. The deliberately broad **3000–500 BCE focus** is not a precise conversion, single migration or end of living languages. The research-region marker is not an origin village, route or boundary. A Ghent BantuFirst page returned 503 and was not counted as inspected evidence.
- `austronesian-early-expansions`: [Peter Bellwood's ANU Press section](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p63701/mobile/ch02s05.html) is an older synthesis with a proposed early Taiwan/Philippine/island sequence; the later [Spriggs research record](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/archaeology-and-the-austronesian-expansion-where-are-we-now/) was inspected only as an abstract and identifies archaeological material as a proxy in testing language-dispersal hypotheses. Neither evidence type proves a single itinerary or identity. The selected **3000–1500 BCE** early window is not an endpoint for communities or languages. Existing Lapita and later Pacific entries remain separately dated.
- `madagascar-comoros-crops-connections`: [Crowther et al. (2016)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1522714113), abstract and selected sections inspected, compare sampled mainland East African, Comorian and Malagasy crop remains from approximately **650–1200 CE**. Their reported Asian rice in eighth- to tenth-century island samples is evidence for connections, not a first-occupation date, one uniform ancestry or a demonstrated route. The island point is a broad context, not a particular excavation. Comparisons with Bantu and later Merina entries do not claim direct descent or political continuity.
