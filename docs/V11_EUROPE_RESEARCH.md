## Disagreements and omissions

- E1 gives 25 February and 1 August for Ukrainka's birth/death; E2 gives 26 February (acknowledging 25) and 15 August. The atlas uses only 1871 and 1913, without inventing a calendar-conversion explanation. E2 also lists the first collection as 1892 in one works list but 1893 in its life chronology. E1's dedicated literary chronology supplies the adopted 1893. Inconsistent Heine translation years and the number of days taken to write *Forest Song* are omitted.
- E2's blanket description of prohibited Ukrainian books is narrower in E3, which explicitly identifies exceptions and censorship/orthography conditions. The connected record follows the dedicated discussion. It does not equate the 1905 revolution with formal repeal of the Ems decree; E3 says it was never formally revoked.
- E4 compresses the seven-part *Consultatio* into an apparent lifetime publication. E5 distinguishes the first two printed volumes from the rest, rediscovered in manuscript in 1935. The biography avoids repeating the compressed claim. Birthplace and the efficacy of the school experiment remain qualified.
- A Britannica page was blocked by browser security. It was not read, bypassed or used as inspected evidence. A Harvard catalogue result redirected to an unrelated publisher page; it was not used. Search results only located sources, and supplied no final claim evidence.

## V10 evidence follow-up

The [Shevchenko Museum biography](https://shevchenko.ca/taras-shevchenko/biography/) was read on 24 October as a bounded follow-up to `person-taras-shevchenko`. Its 1838 emancipation and 1840 *Kobzar* publication support the existing chronology. The arrest/exile section distinguishes the 1847 arrest near Kyiv from subsequent St Petersburg imprisonment; the existing V10 pin correctly labels the capital as interrogation, not arrest. The 1857 release was followed by restrictions on returning to the capital, so release should not be expanded into unrestricted freedom of movement. No V10 correction was needed and no frozen record was rewritten. This check does not verify every museum interpretation or reconstruct the entire exile route.

## Further work

Eastern and southeastern European local social histories remain thin. This contribution adds two connected book/education sequences, rather than pretending to fill that gap with famous names. Useful next research includes Ukrainian publisher/reader archives; Moravian pupil and teacher evidence beyond the reformer's proposals; Ottoman/Balkan multilingual education; and regional specialist review of English/Chinese name and literary-title conventions. No geometry or route leg is added.
