# V9 cross-region critical editorial review

Reviewed 23 October 2026. Scope: 15 deliberately selected high-risk records in the new Europe and Oceania arrays, plus two Africa biographies referred by the integrating editor. This is a targeted editorial audit, not a claim-by-claim verification of either region or the inherited corpus. English and Chinese texts, displayed intervals, milestone descriptions/locations and timeline metadata were compared. Structural validation was used only after historical review; it does not establish factual truth.

## Result and applied corrections

The review distinguishes a mistaken implication from a date that is already appropriately qualified. No polity interval, boundary, culture duration, identity or timeline priority was changed without evidence. Clear corrections were applied to `person-lise-meitner`, `fsm-constitutional-transition`, `person-liliuokalani`, `person-funmilayo-ransome-kuti` and `person-frantz-fanon`. The first three concern chronology labels, citation metadata or representative-location purpose; the Africa changes disclose conflicting dates and replace clinical-setting pins with bibliographically evidenced publication context. Both languages were updated together.

## Africa referrals and fixes

`person-funmilayo-ransome-kuti`: the earlier unqualified statement that the Abeokuta Ladies' Club was founded in 1944 was too certain. [African Feminist Forum](https://www.africanfeministforum.com/funmilayo-ransome-kuti-nigeria/), relevant biography text independently retrieved, describes 1944 expansion to market women and study in England during 1919–1923. [Oxford's biography](https://oxfordaasc.com/display/10.1093/acref/9780195301731.001.0001/acref-9780195301731-e-49815), authoritative publisher index inspected, specifies expansion in March 1944; its full page returned 403. The existing UNESCO/Eldis evidence family instead gives 1922 return and calls 1944 foundation. The summary now describes mid-1940s organizing rather than assigning an uncontested foundation year. The 1922 marker is approximate and explicitly represents an early-1920s return; both date note and milestone disclose the discrepant accounts. The 1944 milestone names organizing/expansion and discloses the foundation-versus-expansion conflict. No unsupported 1932 foundation claim was added. Eldis remains identified as derivative of UNESCO.

`person-frantz-fanon`: both publication milestones previously reused Blida's clinical/political setting. Bibliographic evidence does not justify reading that as where publication occurred or where the author was present. The [BnF catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb32091544r), full record text inspected, gives Paris, Éditions du Seuil, 1952 for *Peau noire, masques blancs*. [UQAM's 1961-book bibliographic page](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/fanon_franz/damnes_de_la_terre/damnes_de_la_terre.html), page text inspected, gives Paris, François Maspero, 1961, separately from its later edition/digitization information. The [1952-book bibliographic page](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/fanon_franz/peau_noire_masques_blancs/peau_noire_masques_blancs.html) also corroborates Paris/Seuil/1952. These are bibliographic checks, not full-book reading. Milestones now use Paris coordinates labeled publication context, explicitly without claiming personal presence. The main biography's representative Algeria pin remains appropriate for the selected clinical/political story.

## Bilingual and timeline checks

The 15 samples preserve the same substantive claims and uncertainty in English and Chinese. The corrected Meitner, Ransome-Kuti and Fanon milestones were checked sentence by sentence across languages. No living Indigenous identity is assigned an extinction date by these sampled trade, heritage or research windows. Representative coordinates remain references, not invented borders or itineraries.

The combined Europe/Oceania arrays contain 68 records: 14 polities (8 major, 6 secondary) and 54 non-polities. Only the political entries carry timeline metadata. Keeping the selected late-Roman, Holy Roman and Tonga overviews major, and narrower/composite phases secondary, is a curatorial decision rather than a historical finding. This review found no factual reason to alter those priorities; the application owner still needs to assess their on-screen density with other regions.

`validateRecords` passes on all three edited arrays. Related IDs and milestones were checked against the available atlas corpus. Those checks show schema/reference consistency only. They do not upgrade indexed sources to full-text evidence or establish exhaustive regional coverage. Remaining important limits include Tonga's approximate endpoint, the conflicting Ransome-Kuti dates, incomplete access to the original 1939 paper, and FSM testimony's internally inconsistent date heading.
