# V12 Atlantic Canada: landscape, displacement and archival lives

Reviewed 24 October 2026 through the root Codex in-app browser. Two original bilingual contextual records accompany the three larger V12 research batches. Actual accessible page text was inspected; linked archive items and heritage management documents were not automatically treated as read.

| Record | Scope and representative location | Inspected evidence and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `grand-pre-dykes-displacement` | 1682–1764, Grand-Pré, Nova Scotia; settlement, dyke agriculture, deportations and permission to return | [Parks Canada, History of Grand-Pré](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/ns/grandpre/culture/histoire-history) supports settlement about 1682, agricultural trade, the 1755 Les Mines deportation, wider deportations continuing to 1762, and permission to return in 1764. [UNESCO, Landscape of Grand Pré](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1404/) supports the polder, aboiteaux, collective maintenance and subsequent agricultural/memorial landscape. The 2012 inscription is not an occupation or construction date. No family-by-family deportation itinerary or exact historical dyke geometry is claimed. |
| `halifax-african-nova-scotian-archives` | 1749–1834 documentary window, Halifax as a colonial port/administrative context | [Nova Scotia Archives exhibit introduction](https://archives.novascotia.ca/africanns/) explicitly defines the archival window and says Black history/slavery predate Halifax's founding. [Book of Negroes introduction](https://archives.novascotia.ca/africanns/book-of-negroes/) supports the 1783 New York evacuation register and different destinations. Halifax is not the register's creation location and does not represent every Black community. The two pages share an archival institution; they are not independent witnesses. Individual register entries, all 100+ exhibited documents and every image were not reviewed. |

The records connect with existing New France, Québec, treaty, Shadd Cary and Freetown histories while retaining distinct date meanings. The return permission does not imply recovery of every former home or the end of displacement. The 1834 endpoint is an archival/legal focus, not the end of African Nova Scotian history. The corresponding files retain bilingual review limits and source-access metadata.
