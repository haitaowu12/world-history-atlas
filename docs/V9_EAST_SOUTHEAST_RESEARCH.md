## Research method and evidence status

This batch used independent web research, including institutional and scholarly search, opening source pages and inspecting relevant passages. It did not consume or claim a Gemini Deep Research result. Sources include Korean institutional and scholarly material, Japanese museums and libraries, Vietnamese museum pages, English-language scholarship, Chinese museum material, archives and international institutions. English and Chinese narratives are original paired syntheses; no outside bilingual expert review is claimed.

Each source carries its own `retrievalStatus` and evidence note. “Page text inspected” means relevant published page passages were inspected, not that every linked object, archival file or full book was read. A scholarly abstract, chapter summary, catalog description or search-indexed passage is identified as such. A failed full-page retrieval has not been relabeled as a successful reading. Source URLs were discovered from returned results or followed from inspected institutional pages, not constructed as plausible citations.

Several entries rely on one institution or source family. Those records carry an `expansion.evidenceLimit`; two pages from one museum are not independent corroboration. The two Wonhyo scholarly items also share an author and are flagged. Some additional references supply broad contextual evidence rather than independently supporting every biographical assertion; the date notes and source notes identify examples such as Yosano and Đổi Mới. More independent verification would strengthen these rows.

## Historical decisions and corrections during research

- **Origins and period labels:** early Goguryeo and Baekje start at a consolidation focus around 300 rather than turning traditional foundations into precise archaeological facts. Gaya is a collection of autonomous polities. Kofun/Asuka schemes overlap, and an Asuka or Nara court did not administer every region identically.
- **Later Silla and Goryeo:** “Unified” is qualified to preserve the simultaneous northern Balhae kingdom. Goryeo’s 918 foundation is separated from Silla’s 935 surrender and the 936 consolidation, avoiding the Met chronology’s compressed suggestion of immediate reunification in 918.
- **Source errors:** the Columbia Vietnam timeline includes a wrong early Tang foundation date and broad statements about Champa; those are not adopted. The Vietnamese Lý–Trần museum text calls the dynasty fought in 1077 Tang; that erroneous reference is not repeated. A typographic year in the Nguyễn Du biography is not adopted. Identifying an institution does not make every sentence in its pages authoritative.
- **Lê continuity:** Early Lê (1428–1527), Restored Lê (1533–1788) and Trịnh–Nguyễn regional government are separate records. The terminal 1788 convention follows the Vietnamese museum; precise final court collapse and later claims can be framed differently. The restored name does not imply one executive authority or exclude overlapping Mạc claims.
- **Brunei:** a dedicated precolonial Kota Batu court record was added using the original shipwreck study, official museum description and Chinese institutional documentation of the 1408 royal visit. The court record uses an explicit 1400–1600 focus within a wider fourteenth–seventeenth-century setting; no full kingdom foundation/end dates or historical empire polygon are inferred. The wreck paper favors approximately 1500–1510, while the official museum overview uses a broad fifteenth-century description; the entry does not assert an exact sinking year.
- **Ryukyu and Lan Xang:** political records use qualified focus intervals. Ryukyu’s 1401–1600 bar is not a claim that the kingdom began or ended in those years. Lan Xang’s 1401–1600 focus is likewise not its complete lifespan. The timeline must retain these date notes.
- **Tokugawa:** regulated external relationships are not complete isolation. The 1603 institutional beginning and the 1615 art-historical convention are distinguished. Nagasaki/Dejima is one channel, not the entirety of Japan’s foreign relations.
- **People:** some person rows concern a documented career or reign rather than full lifespan (Sei Shōnagon, Seondeok, Tun Sri Lanang and Jayavarman VII). No invented birth or death is supplied. Kim Dae-jung follows the Nobel facts page’s 1924 date while noting varying early-date conventions. Birth milestones for Fukuzawa and Nguyễn Du use separate representative locations from their later institutions. Zanabazar’s 1635 marker explicitly uses a collection reference rather than claiming an Ulaanbaatar birthplace; his 1691 Beijing milestone has a distinct location.
- **Modern violence:** no single casualty totals are asserted for Sook Ching, Gwangju, Hiroshima or the Indonesian killings. State, military, diplomatic and commemorative sources have different perspectives. War endpoints do not imply an end to displacement, ordnance danger or social harm.
- **Political categories:** the Trịnh–Nguyễn comparison and Aceh sultanah historiography are contextual records, not extra primary-timeline state bars. Events, texts, culture and people are not promoted to political entities merely to fill space.

## Retrieval limitations

Full-page retrieval failed or was insufficient for some British Museum pages/PDF, the Wonhyo chapter and logic page, some Nobel pages, the Hikayat Aceh nomination, the Laguna original commentary, some UN pages and the Asian Civilisations Museum exhibition page. Where institutional search-indexed content was used, sources say so. The Chinese National Museum legacy page opened with encoding problems; the Chinese indexed excerpt supplied the dated royal visit. The Cambridge Dutton chapter was inspected through its published summary; no access to the full paid chapter is claimed. The detailed ARI Burma/Siam paper and Brunei shipwreck paper were readable as PDFs. The former returned a misleading extractor title; its internal pages identify ARI Working Paper 64 and the relevant study.

## Coverage remaining

These bounded batches do not exhaust East or Southeast Asian history, verify all inherited China material or reconstruct historical boundaries. More depth is still needed for local Korean society, early Ryukyu, regional and upland Southeast Asia, the Philippine archipelago beyond Luzon, Ainu experiences across several present jurisdictions, women outside documented elites, colonial economies in multiple countries and long post-1990 histories. The new biographies include five women; further expansion should avoid relying on rulers and famous intellectuals alone. No new journey legs or territorial geometries are asserted.
