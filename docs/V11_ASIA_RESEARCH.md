# V11 Asia: learning, print and organization

Checked **2026-10-24** in a browser. Applies only to `dist/data/expansion-asia-v11.json`. This is an editorial source synthesis, not independent specialist or bilingual scholarly acceptance.

## Delivered scope

Eight new bilingual records form four pairs: **four biographies with sixteen dated milestones** and **four connected institutional/cultural histories**. The selection contributes four records to South Asia (Bangladesh and Sri Lanka), two to non-China East Asia (Japan), and two to Southeast Asia (Indonesia). The three existing workstreams are `SA3`, `EA3` and `SE3`. No political timeline bars, route legs or geometry are added. English biographies contain 235–237 words; context narratives contain 180–184 words. Chinese text preserves the chronology and qualifications.

The **eleven distinct cited URLs** had their relevant text opened and inspected. They are not eleven independent evidentiary traditions. Banglapedia’s two entries share a publisher; Aisyiyah’s two pages share an institutional history, and the provincial commemoration may repeat parts of that history. The South Asian Britain database and AISLS offer different scholarly contexts, but neither substitutes for reading all the named archives. Princeton’s catalogue description overlaps the AISLS digitization project and is not independent confirmation. The National Diet Library profile and State Library Victoria collection interpretation are separate institutional summaries, not a complete primary-source study of Seitō.

Stable IDs and source metadata are new. V9 and V10 source arrays, manifests and research ledgers are unchanged. Links to existing people and histories connect the new material to earlier learning paths instead of claiming to replace their evidence.

## Inspected-source ledger

All dates below are the source-review date, **2026-10-24**. `inspected-text` means the relevant webpage text was read in a browser, not that all underlying archives or linked books were read.

Biographical narrative and literary/public-work chronology inspected. The source dates organizing to 1969 while the organization page and self-description date formal emergence to 1970. The new entry distinguishes mobilization from formal organization instead of silently homogenizing these dates. Praiseful assessments, first-Muslim-woman federation superlatives and lists of awards are not needed for this selected story.
- **S02 — [Bangladesh Mahila Parishad](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Bangladesh_Mahila_Parishad)**, Nibedita Das Purkayastha / Banglapedia. ## Conflicts, prior limitations and held follow-up

1. **V10 source recheck — Philippine suffrage statement:** the [PCW 78th-anniversary statement](https://pcw.gov.ph/pcw-statement-on-the-78th-anniversary-of-womens-suffrage-in-the-philippines/) still returned a security-verification page in a browser. The historical V10 `inspected-search-indexed-text` label remains limited; this contribution does **not** claim to have now read its full body. The 1936 act already cited in V10 is a distinct type of evidence from the plebiscite outcome. Full outcome documentation remains open.
2. **Aisyiyah preschool:** S09 dates Frobel School to 1919; S08 gives 1922. Both come from the same institution, so this is a real unresolved internal chronology conflict, not independent confirmation. Both languages avoid an exact preschool year; the separate 1922 women’s prayer-space date in S09 is not substituted for the school date. An archival institutional history is needed before a preschool milestone can be added.
3. **Coomaraswamy access leads:** initial same-tab navigation to Princeton stalled, but a separately opened catalogue tab was subsequently found and its metadata and summary inspected (S11). The embedded viewer was present; individual photographs were not systematically reviewed. The USC Scalar art-history page `https://scalar.usc.edu/anvc/the-art-bulletin/about-coomaraswamy` failed with an HTTP/2 error. An attempted subsequent art-historian profile navigation did not produce a usable body. No claims were taken from those attempts or promoted from search snippets. The cited scholarly pages and catalogue suffice for the selected, restrained chronology, but fuller intellectual biography and collection provenance remain future work.
4. **Distinct histories:** the craft entry addresses how documentation selected and represented makers, rather than repeating Coomaraswamy’s career; the other contexts address collective print or organizing mechanisms rather than making a second biography. Existing Ceylon plantation/citizenship history, Bengali language/constitutional history, Japanese women’s education and Indonesian youth/education records provide comparison, not automatic causal proof.
5. **Open review:** Bengali, Sinhala/Tamil, Japanese and Indonesian specialist checks would strengthen interpretation. A fully reviewed archive, an institution’s web summary and an organizational self-description are different evidence levels. No claim of parity with China, exhaustive regional representation or human learning effectiveness is made.
