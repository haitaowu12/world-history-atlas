# V5 world-history expansion: Africa, Europe and the Americas

Research checked: **23 October 2026 (UTC)**. Integrated dataset: the 36 IDs listed below in [`history.json`](../dist/data/history.json).

## Deliverable and scope

This package adds **36 original bilingual records**, with **9 each** for Africa, Europe, North America and Latin America. It contains **72 distinct, readable source URLs**, two per record. Each source object includes a claim-specific `evidenceNote` and `checkedAt`; every record includes English and Chinese titles, summaries, date notes and two key facts. English summaries range from **100 to 109 words**.

The selection adds connected steps and material evidence beneath existing broad entries: Nok and Ife art; Aksum, Lalibela and Gondar; Roman citizenship and Justinian's law; medieval urban record-keeping and maritime exchange; Indigenous villages, diplomacy and forced removal; Maya urbanism and astronomical manuscripts; Andean mining and labor; and constitutional, transport and capital-building projects. It is a focused addition, not a claim that these regions now have exhaustive coverage or the same density as the China reference project.

Theme distribution: **9 states, 8 culture, 7 cities, 6 exchange and 6 knowledge**. All `relatedIds` resolve to an existing curated history/person record or another record in this package. The completed research batch was subsequently integrated into v5.

## Source method and limits

All shipped sources were opened through browsing and relevant readable text, primary-document material or institutional explanations were inspected. Search snippets alone were not counted as verification. Sources are museums, heritage authorities, archives, national parks, universities, parliamentary records and government repositories. The text is original synthesis; no extended quotations or source-page prose was copied.

“Two sources” means two different documents or pages, not necessarily two independent research teams. Some records pair a UNESCO site assessment with its advisory or periodic report, or two pages from the same museum or archive. These pairs provide complementary evidence and context; they are not described as independent replication. `evidenceNote` identifies whether the cited material is primary research/documentation, a holding institution's description or a modern institutional interpretation.

The original documents behind several entries are directly available: the Roman citizenship papyrus through its nomination dossier; the Little Domesday transcription; the Royal Proclamation; the U.S. Declaration of Independence; the 1848 Seneca Falls convention report; the 1838 parliamentary debate; the 1917 Mexican official gazette; and the 1021 Norse wood-dating research article. A modern explanatory page is not silently equated with the underlying primary source.

Government accounts of colonial treaties, Indigenous peoples, emancipation and national infrastructure are read critically. Crown claims are labeled as claims; legislation is separated from lived implementation; Indigenous communities are not said to have disappeared; and changing alliances are not reduced to passive participation in European histories.

Coordinates are rounded **representative points**. They identify a city, monument, regional reference or negotiation setting. They are not surveyed event coordinates, territorial polygons, trade-route extents or assertions that an entire population occupied one point.

## Chronological decisions retained in the records

- **Nok:** c. 900–400 BCE is the Middle Nok/figurative-art flourishing phase, while the wider culture began around 1500 BCE and continued after the displayed phase. The source is the university's stable direct article, replacing the shifting press-archive page.
- **Djenné-Djeno:** UNESCO's rounded c. 250 BCE beginning and the field report's c. 200 BCE–1400 CE occupation span are disclosed rather than falsely reconciled into an exact foundation year. The old mound and current town are distinct.
- **Aksum:** the fourth-century window preserves uncertainty about Ezana's conversion; it is not a century-long ceremony. Coin evidence and church traditions are distinguished.
- **Mapungubwe:** 1220–1300 is the hill phase given by SANParks, not the entire landscape's history. The point is in South Africa.
- **Lalibela:** a twelfth–thirteenth-century context avoids giving every church the same exact royal construction dates.
- **Gondar:** 1636 marks establishment of a permanent capital, not completion of all later palaces.
- **Abu Simbel:** 1968 dates reopening after relocation. The larger Nubian campaign continued from 1960 to 1980.
- **Caracalla:** 212–213 follows UNESCO's 212/213 dating. Rome is the imperial reference point, while the surviving papyrus came from Egypt.
- **Justinian/Hagia Sophia:** law compilation (529–534), initial cathedral construction (532–537) and later dome rebuilding remain separate events.
- **Aachen:** the 793–813 construction interval comes from UNESCO's detailed assessment; rounded “around 800” summaries are not treated as a different exact chronology.
- **Domesday:** 1086 is the survey year, although entries describe earlier ownership and values as well. Dunwich is a recorded example, not the compilation headquarters.
- **Norman Palermo/Lübeck:** displayed intervals frame architectural or commercial prominence, not the full life of a city or every institution.
- **British emancipation:** 1833 legislation, 1834 formal emancipation and 1838 termination of apprenticeship are distinct; implementation and exceptions varied.
- **L’Anse aux Meadows:** 1021 dates wood cutting, not the first arrival or entire occupation. Earlier Indigenous history is explicit.
- **Mesa Verde:** c. 1190–1280 is a rounded cliff-dwelling phase within a much longer settlement history. Migration is not disappearance.
- **Québec/Niagara:** foundation, military alliance, proclamation and negotiation are separate steps, with map pins tied to the stated role.
- **U.S. Declaration:** the independence vote (2 July), adoption (4 July) and most signatures (2 August 1776) remain distinct.
- **Cherokee removal:** 1838–1839 refers to the principal forced-removal phase, not all earlier movement or later rebuilding. No falsely precise death total is given.
- **CPR:** 7 November 1885 marks the last-spike/track-completion ceremony, not all later passenger-service milestones.
- **Monte Albán:** c. 500 BCE–850 CE is a principal urban span, with the 800/850 endpoint variation disclosed. Zapotec history continues beyond the capital's decline.
- **Nasca/Palpa:** the broad c. 500 BCE–500 CE tradition contains different phases. No single ritual or astronomical hypothesis is asserted as settled for every figure.
- **Palenque:** Pakal's reign (615–683) frames the building program; the Temple of the Inscriptions was completed under his successor.
- **Dresden Codex:** 1250 is explicitly approximate, and northern Yucatán is a proposed origin, not an identified workshop.
- **Tenochtitlan:** 1521 dates the siege's culmination, not all conquest, resistance or the end of Indigenous agency.
- **Potosí/Cartagena:** 1600 and 1700 are clearly identified reading-window endpoints for selected growth phases, not mine closure or final completion of defenses.
- **Mexico 1917:** the original constitutional document is separated from later amendments and from the actual implementation of social rights.
- **Brasília:** construction, plan competition and institutional transfer are separated; later building continued after 1960, and the region is not described as empty of prior inhabitants.

## Source-quality corrections and excluded leads

- The shifting Goethe press archive (`?page=38`) was replaced by the readable direct article at `https://www.uni-frankfurt.de/74990848/Nok_research_project_enters_final_phase` after the root review identified link stability as a risk. Its body explicitly gives the c. 900–400 BCE Middle Nok phase.
- An earlier guessed Goethe `/en/english/` URL returned an error and was not shipped.
- Nature's publisher page and the PMC version of the Norse study were blocked or presented a verification wall in this session. The University of Groningen repository's readable publisher PDF is shipped instead.
- Several National Archives HTML pages were blocked. Readable official education/palaeography PDFs provide the Domesday evidence; fictional classroom role-play passages are not used as historical facts.
- A proposed Toledo translation entry was dropped because the intended university sources could not be read. Norman Palermo uses accessible primary institutional material instead.
- The SLUB flyer contains outdated framing about the number of surviving Maya codices and other broad historical claims. These were excluded; the entry uses the manuscript description, approximate date, proposed origin and contents only.
- The Library of Congress page on Mexico's 1917 constitution includes an erroneous total article count. That count is excluded. The original official-gazette facsimile is also cited.
- Older heritage descriptions sometimes use superlatives, “empty land” rhetoric or colonial assumptions. Those are not adopted merely because the publisher is authoritative.
- Uncertain Nok ironworking start dates, exact Tenochtitlan troop totals, precise Cherokee mortality and large historical Potosí population totals were omitted because they are not necessary to the learning goals and require more specialized treatment.

## Verified source ledger

Each listed URL was readable and inspected in this session. Checked date: 2026-10-23 UTC. The notes identify the claims used, source type and limits.

### Nok: farmers and terracotta makers

Record: `nok-terracotta-workshops`.

1. [Nok research project enters final phase](https://www.uni-frankfurt.de/74990848/Nok_research_project_enters_final_phase) — Goethe University Frankfurt. Direct research-institution article describes Nigerian–German fieldwork, culture from c. 1500 BCE, pearl-millet farming and the Middle Nok phase c. 900–400 BCE. Checked 2026-10-23.

2. [Arts of Africa: Inside the Exhibition — Nok](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/arts-of-africa/inside-the-exhibition) — The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Museum interpretation describes figurative forms and features, and places the decline of the Nok tradition around 400 BCE; functions are not treated as certain. Checked 2026-10-23.

### Djenné-Djeno: a city in the inland Niger delta

Record: `djenne-djeno-urbanism`.

1. [Old Towns of Djenné](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/116/) — UNESCO World Heritage Centre. Institutional heritage synthesis dates regional settlement to c. 250 BCE and distinguishes the present town and archaeological sites. Checked 2026-10-23.

2. [Results of recent excavations at Jenné-jeno and Djenné, Mali](https://anthropology.rice.edu/sites/g/files/bxs3341/files/inline-files/djennemuseumsite.pdf) — Susan Keech McIntosh and Roderick McIntosh / Rice University. Primary archaeological research report gives c. 200 BCE–1400 CE occupation, decline after c. 1200, pottery/core methods and limits on interpreting links with modern Djenné. Checked 2026-10-23.

### Aksum: royal conversion and Christian coinage

Record: `aksum-ezana-christianity`.

1. [Foundations of Aksumite Civilization and Its Christian Legacy](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/foundations-of-aksumite-civilization-and-its-christian-legacy-1st-7th-century) — The Metropolitan Museum of Art. ### Mapungubwe: farming, trade and a hilltop court
