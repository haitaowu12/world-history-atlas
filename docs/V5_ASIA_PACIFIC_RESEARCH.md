# World History Atlas v5 — Asia and Pacific research batch

Research checked: **23 October 2026 (UTC)**.

Integrated dataset: the 40 IDs listed below in [`history.json`](../dist/data/history.json). This batch contains **40 new records**, with **8 each** for South Asia, Southeast Asia, Oceania, West/Central Asia, and Japan/Korea within East Asia. It is an expansion batch, not a claim of comprehensive worldwide parity with the Chinese reference project. The completed research batch was subsequently integrated into v5.

## Method and scope

The existing `dist/data/history.json` and `people.json` were examined before choosing topics. The new material adds dated developments within existing regional histories: urban infrastructure, art and religious institutions, knowledge production, port transformations, constitutional change, Indigenous authority and land rights. It does not duplicate the mainland Chinese dynastic events being imported separately.

Each record has an original English summary of **96–106 words**, a substantive Chinese summary, two key facts in both languages, a bilingual date note, a representative map point, related records, and evidence notes for its sources. The English summaries total **4,053 words**. There are **81 source references to 81 distinct URLs**: two per record except the Japanese constitution entry, which uses three complementary archival sources. All 81 final source URLs supplied substantive retrieved text during this research. An access date records that consultation; it is not a claim that every statement on a source page has been accepted.

Primary documents, institutional object catalogues, archaeological project reports, national museums and archives, official heritage agencies, UNESCO/ICOMOS, and university research were prioritized. General-purpose encyclopedias and commercial history summaries were not retained. Sources were read for the particular claims stated in each evidence note; whole books or long PDFs were not represented as read cover to cover. The short translations and summaries are original writing rather than copied institutional descriptions.

Coordinates are representative editorial anchors within the named public site or region. They are not surveyed historical border vertices or claims of exact locations for every activity in a narrative. Event dates, reign-focused intervals, approximate archaeological horizons, and later conservation history are distinguished in `dateNote`. All intervals are closed, historical intervals; none is marked ongoing. No record has a year zero.

## Editorial decisions and source conflicts

1. **Taxila:** Sirkap’s selected urban interval is distinct from the full sequence of Taxila settlements and from broad claims about a timeless “university.” Museum evidence for contacts is not interpreted as proof of territorial rule.
2. **Ajanta:** The two principal phases are not described as continuous construction. Fifth-century Vakataka patronage is distinguished from the broad art-historical label “Gupta period.”
3. **Thanjavur:** UNESCO’s detailed synthesis distinguishes likely initiation in 1003–1004 from consecration in 1009–1010. ASI has inconsistent regnal-year wording, so that wording is not reproduced. The entry is separate from Gangaikondacholapuram’s 1035 temple.
4. **Fatehpur Sikri:** The broader 1571 development convention and ASI’s 1572 court move are both identified. An abandonment explanation based solely on water shortage is not asserted.
5. **Bengali language movement:** The 1952 killings are a focal event, not the start of the entire movement. Retrospective wording that calls the event the beginning of the movement is not adopted. East Pakistan is distinguished from later independent Bangladesh.
6. **Đông Sơn and Pyu:** A museum object’s date range and the combined chronology of several archaeological cities are explicitly labelled. Neither is converted into one precise political lifespan or empire boundary.
7. **Siam, 1932:** A royal-centred institutional biography is read alongside the Pridi Institute’s documentary discussion. The 24 June intervention, 27 June interim charter and 10 December permanent constitution remain separate. A completed democratic transition is not asserted.
8. **Singapore:** The 1819 trading-post agreement is not confused with the separate 1824 sovereignty cession. The Malay signatories remain visible in the narrative.
9. **Philippines, 1898:** The declaration is distinct from the later First Republic and the 1946 settlement. NHCP’s retrospective title for Aguinaldo is not used to date the First Republic to June 1898. The U.S. Archives article’s misleading shorthand about the war ending at Aguinaldo’s capture is not used.
10. **Wairau Bar:** The point date 1300 is explicitly approximate. The narrative includes Rangitāne involvement and the context of ancestral repatriation, while keeping the map point regional rather than identifying individual burial locations.
11. **Lapaha:** The 2014 primary archaeological study places the move from Heketa about 1350–1400; an older ICOMOS synthesis relates an earlier traditional foundation chronology. A selected 1350–1500 phase is used, not an exact claim of foundation or demise. Imported adzes demonstrate interaction without proving uniform annexation of their source islands.
12. **Marshall Islands:** The short UNESCO Bikini site introduction conflates a regional test total with a single-atoll total. The adopted UNESCO inscription decision and U.S. National Park Service account resolve the scope: **67 at Bikini and Enewetak together, 23 at Bikini**. The incorrect short introduction is excluded from the final sources. Displacement and contamination do not end at the timeline endpoint of 1958.
13. **Cook Islands:** The event is self-government in free association in 1965, not a generic independence declaration. The 1965 allocation of responsibilities is not projected unchanged into 2026.
14. **Vanuatu:** The event is ni-Vanuatu independence on 30 July 1980. The Electoral Office’s stale assertion that no referendum had ever occurred, and unrelated unreliable early-European chronology on the government overview, are not reproduced.
15. **Mabo:** The point is Mer, the land at the centre of the case, while the date note identifies Canberra as the court venue. Recognition of continuing title is not described as unconditional return of all land.
16. **Nineveh:** The seventh-century context is not a precise foundation date or a disputed royal reign span. Clay fragments are not counted as complete books. The source describes a collection assembled through several processes and generations.
17. **Sardis:** Approximate late-seventh-century development is distinguished from an exact invention date. Coinage is not equated with the invention of all money, and minting continues after the selected Lydian-phase endpoint.
18. **Cyrus and Bisotun:** Royal inscriptions are treated as political claims and evidence of self-presentation. The Cyrus Cylinder is not labelled the first universal human-rights charter. UNESCO’s imprecise Darius accession shorthand is not repeated; the selected interval concerns the early monument and victory narrative.
19. **Damascus and Isfahan:** Construction windows and individual building dates are distinguished from the age of all surviving fabric. The questionable “15th-century Timurid palace” wording in UNESCO’s short Isfahan introduction is not reused; the detailed synthesis supplies the actual ensemble context.
20. **Hōryūji and Nara:** Foundation traditions, the 670 fire, rebuilding and the Eastern Precinct’s 739 foundation are distinct. Nara’s 740–745 interruption is explicitly retained rather than flattening 710–784 into uninterrupted residence.
21. **Tripitaka Koreana:** The 1236–1251 interval identifies the office-to-completion project chronology in the nomination’s production account; narrower carving dates appear elsewhere in the same dossier and heritage summaries. The pin is **Ganghwa**, the supervising-office reference, not the later Haeinsa repository. The 1398 transfer is explicit.
22. **Jikji:** The superlative is restricted to surviving evidence of movable metal-type printing. Earlier uses are acknowledged, and no undocumented transmission to Gutenberg is claimed.
23. **Tokugawa government:** Sekigahara in 1600, appointment in 1603 and Osaka consolidation in 1615 remain distinct. Immediate universal peace in 1603 is not inferred.
24. **Japanese constitution and South Korea, 1987:** Promulgation and commencement are separate dates. Civic mobilization, concessions and implementation are separated; a declaration is not treated as the completion of every democratic objective.

## Access limitations handled

Several candidate pages could not supply a usable body and were excluded from the final dataset: British Museum object/blog pages for the Cyrus Cylinder and Ashurbanipal; several ORACC hosts and mirrors; an ANU repository download of the Lapaha paper; Hōryūji’s own English HTML/PDF; the Korean Heritage Service Tripitaka URL; a Korea Democracy Foundation PDF; an obsolete Japanese Cabinet constitution URL; Edo-Tokyo Museum’s main exhibition page; a New Zealand foreign-affairs Cook Islands page; the Tamil HRCE temple page; a blocked Singapore Roots object page; and the NCCA Philippine–American War page. Search-index excerpts from those failed pages were not silently marked as successfully read full pages.

Usable alternatives included the open PNAS paper at PMC, the DFG project report and UCL research page, Nara Prefecture’s preservation account, the UNESCO documentary nomination, National Diet Library archival texts, Tokyo Museum Collection’s object page, and other retrieved institutional records. The old UNESCO war-diary URL redirected to a generic page; the current item-specific URL was retrieved and retained instead.
