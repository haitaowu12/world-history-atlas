# V11 Americas and Oceania: people within institutions

Checked **24 October 2026**. Data: `dist/data/expansion-americas-oceania-v11.json`.

## Scope

Eight new bilingual entries: four biographies and four connected histories, with **19 dated biographical milestones**. Two records concern North America, two Jamaica/Caribbean history and four Oceania. Three biographies concern women, including Papua New Guinean lawyer and diplomat Meg Taylor. English biographies contain 228–238 words and context narratives 179–182 words; Chinese narratives preserve the same focus and qualifications. These figures describe a bounded contribution, not regional parity or representative sampling.

The pairings develop existing reading contexts:

- Peratrovich and Alaska's public-accommodation law link earlier Tlingit resistance and U.S. civil-rights history to sustained Native women's organizing.
- Bennett-Coverley and Jamaican broadcasting connect everyday speech, cultural work and migration with the existing Claudia Jones and Garifuna histories.
- Taylor and the Boe Declaration add a Papua New Guinean woman's legal/diplomatic career and a later regional-policy episode to existing independence and Forum-foundation entries.
- Tjibaou and the Mélanésia 2000–ADCK sequence connect Kanak cultural work and institutional development to the existing 1878 resistance entry, without asserting political consensus or creating new territorial geometry.

All IDs are new and every related ID was checked against the V10 inventory or this addition. None adds a political-timeline bar. V10 and earlier source files are unchanged. Taylor's 1974–2019 span is an explicitly selected career, not a lifespan or a claim about her current offices. These records use release 5, edition `v11`, and existing workstreams NA1, NA3, LA4 and OC3.

## Method and source access

Research used the Codex in-app browser (`iab`) for discovery and direct inspection of relevant source page bodies. Fourteen distinct supporting URLs were inspected. Search snippets and automated overviews were used only to locate sources, not as claim evidence. Source metadata identifies inspected claim/date support and access type. Selected document descriptions and finding aids are not described as complete archival-file inspection. Original bilingual prose is synthesis, not copied institutional language.

Coordinates are rounded representative reference points. Sources establish places and roles, not survey coordinates. A pin can indicate a study city, legislature, institutional office or regional setting rather than presence on a publication day. Milestones do not supply a continuous travel route. Approximation of geography does not make an otherwise supported year approximate.

Two Forum URLs are one institutional family. Their value is primary evidence of a decision, speech or declared intention; they do not independently prove implementation or outcomes. Commemorative museum, university and library narratives also require comparison and cannot establish unanimous community endorsement.

## Inspected supporting-source register

### Elizabeth Peratrovich and the Alaska legislation

1. [Anchorage Museum: Elizabeth Peratrovich](https://www.anchoragemuseum.org/exhibits/extra-tough-women-of-the-north/women-of-the-north-profiles/elizabeth-peratrovich-major-force-behind-alaskas-anti-discrimination-bill/) — `inspected-text`. Supports Tlingit Lukaax.ádi/Raven identity, 1911–1958 lifespan, Sisterhood presidency, 1945 intervention and 16 February signing. The page's broad first-currency claim is not used.
2. [Richard F. Weingroff / FHWA: Who Is Elizabeth Peratrovich?](https://highways.dot.gov/highway-history/general-highway-history/who-elizabeth-peratrovich-story-behind-countrys-first-anti) — `inspected-text`. Supports 1911 Petersburg birth, racial restrictions, 1933 marriage, late-1941 Juneau move and letter, 1943 failure, continued mobilization, 1945 testimony and 1958 death. The source's nationwide first-law and commemorative-year descriptions are not adopted.
3. [Alaska State Archives: Anti-Discrimination Act of 1945](https://archives.alaska.gov/education/peratrovich.html) — `inspected-text` of explanatory narrative and archival descriptions. Supports ANB/ANS collective pressure, 1943 failed proposal, 1945 House Bill 14, penalties, dated bill/voting-record descriptions and the later commemoration. Original linked scans were not separately analyzed.

**Milestones:** 1911 Petersburg birth (FHWA); 1941 letter/Juneau organizing (FHWA); 1943 legislative setback as a campaign event, not proof of personal attendance (FHWA and Archives); 1945 Senate intervention and law (all three). The 1911–1958 biography does not require a death-place pin: no death address was inferred.

**Conflicts/omissions:** The [University of Alaska student essay](https://www.alaska.edu/uajourney/notable-people/juneau/elizabeth-peratrovich/) was inspected during discovery but is not a supporting data source. Its relocation chronology and 1998 commemorative date do not align with the fuller institutional accounts (Archives gives 1988). ANS founding appears as 1915 in the museum profile and 1914 in the [NPS historian's article](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/dena-history-peratrovich.htm), also inspected. No founding year is assigned. Universal claims that this was the first anti-discrimination law anywhere in the United States are omitted. The law's enactment is not a claim of immediate compliance or the end of discrimination.

### Louise Bennett-Coverley and Jamaican speech in public culture

4. [National Library of Jamaica: Louise Bennett Coverley](https://nlj.gov.jm/project/rt-hon-dr-louise-bennett-coverley-1919-2006/) — `inspected-text`. Supports birth/family occupations, 1945 scholarship, BBC work, 1953 New York, 1954 marriage, 1955 return, Welfare Commission village/drama work, 1987 Canada migration and 2006 death.
5. [The National Archives: Louise Bennett-Coverley](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/louise-bennett-coverley/) — `inspected-text`. Curated narrative includes selected application/transcript material, 1945 RADA study, a 1946 RADA report and outbound passenger-list description, BBC programmes, 1965–1982 `Miss Lou's Views`, Patois and teaching. No claim to have reviewed the complete BW 84/39/17 file or all broadcasts.
6. [McMaster University: Louise Bennett Coverley fonds RC0037](https://archives.mcmaster.ca/index.php/louise-bennett-coverley-fonds) — `inspected-catalogue-text`, including expanded biography and scope note. Supports 1919 birth, 1945 study, 1950 BBC work, 1953/1954/1955 sequence, language advocacy and 2006 death. The fonds dates 1941–2008 describe records, not her lifespan.

**Milestones:** 1919 Kingston birth (NLJ and McMaster); 1945 London study/BBC (NLJ, TNA, McMaster); 1954 marriage in New York (NLJ and McMaster); 1955 return (NLJ and McMaster); 1965 programme start (TNA's explicitly dated 1965–1982 programme run). The later 1987 Canadian move is supported by NLJ and remains in prose; no unsupported Canadian settlement-city pin is supplied.

**Conflicts/omissions:** NLJ and TNA describe Fabian's kinship differently; specific biological/adoptive family claims are omitted. NLJ's list gives a late `Jamaica Labrish` date; no first-publication year for that work is adopted. McMaster associates broadcasting with the 1955 return without defining a programme start, while TNA explicitly dates `Miss Lou's Views` 1965–1982. The context therefore labels that as a documented programme window rather than the origin of Jamaican-language radio. The language is called Jamaican Patois or Jamaican speech, with no suggestion that Bennett invented it or that colonial institutions created the oral tradition. Audience size and educational effectiveness are not quantified.

### Meg Taylor and Pacific regional security

7. [United Nations DESA: Dame Meg Taylor](https://sdgs.un.org/panelists/dame-meg-taylor-29544) — `inspected-text`. Supports legal education/practice, 1974 Somare service, 1989–1994 diplomacy, 1999–2014 CAO and late-2014 Suva role. Its present-tense posts are historical speaker-biography information, not current-2026 office claims.
8. [PIFS: First Female Secretary General announcement](https://forumsec.org/publications/png-s-dame-meg-taylor-will-be-pifs-first-female-secretary-general) — `inspected-text`, dated 1 August 2014, reporting selection in Palau on 31 July. Selection and taking office later in 2014 remain distinct.
9. [Lowy Institute: Dame Meg Taylor](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/people/dame-meg-taylor) — `inspected-text`. Supports public-solicitor/private practice, Law Reform Commission, ambassadorship, 1999 CAO establishment, purpose and August 2014 departure. Promotional claims of globally replicated effectiveness are not adopted.
10. [PIFS: Taylor's Boe Action Plan launch remarks](https://forumsec.org/publications/remarks-secretary-general-dame-meg-taylor-launch-boe-declaration-action-plan) — `inspected-text`. Body dates the Suva speech 15 October 2019; page publication is 16 October. Supports personal role, 2018 declaration, 2019 Tuvalu endorsement, launch and proposed annual review. The text's historical/causal generalizations outside this scope are not adopted.
11. [PIFS: Boe Declaration on Regional Security](https://forumsec.org/publications/boe-declaration-regional-security) — full HTML declaration text inspected, dated 5 September 2018. Supports the expanded security agenda, climate priority, human/environmental/resource security, transnational crime, cybersecurity, sovereignty, national capacity and cooperation commitments.

**Milestones:** 1974 Somare service (UN); 1989 ambassadorship, Washington posting (UN and Lowy); 1999 CAO (UN and Lowy); 2014 selection and later Suva role (UN/PIFS); 2019 Suva launch (dated speech). No birth date is invented. The biography's 2019 end is editorial and does not imply retirement or death.

**Interpretive limit:** Neither Taylor nor the Secretariat is assigned sole authorship of leaders' collective commitments. The Boe context dates decisions and a launch, not funding, completion or measured improvement. Both primary declaration/speech sources share PIFS. No current security advice, country ranking or present political-office assertion is made.

### Jean-Marie Tjibaou and cultural institutions

12. [BnF Passerelles: Jean-Marie Tjibaou et la renaissance culturelle kanak](https://passerelles.essentiels.bnf.fr/fr/chronologie/construction/b4a12a90-c932-45d0-8775-c9e6125a184b-centre-culturel-tjibaou-noumea/article/20bbea29-2ac9-4e2a-bbea-2ca3bc96eab8-jean-marie-tjibaou-et-renaissance-culturelle-kanak) — `inspected-text`, French. Supports 1936–1989, 1975 festival, 1984–1989 FLNKS leadership, 26 June 1988 Matignon, and assassination during Ouvéa mourning ceremonies.
13. [UCLy: Jean-Marie Tjibaou](https://www.ucly.fr/jean-marie-tjibaou/) — `inspected-text`, French institutional alumni account. Supports 1965 ordination, 1968–1970 Lyon study, unfinished ethnology doctorate, 1972 departure from priesthood, 1988 negotiations and 1989 assassination. Its commemorative peacemaker framing is not a complete account of a divided political movement.
14. [ADCK / Centre culturel Tjibaou: Histoire](https://centretjibaou.nc/histoire) — `inspected-text`, French. The older `adck.nc` domain redirects to the centre's current domain. Supports September 1975 festival, Tjibaou and Philippe Missotte, Grande Terre/Loyalty delegations and Téa Kanaké performance, 1989 ADCK formation, 4 May 1998 centre opening, and 2012 transfer. Later institutional stages are context, not Tjibaou's personal milestones.

**Milestones:** 1965 ordination (UCLy; pin is explicitly regional New Caledonia, not an ordination venue); 1968 beginning of Lyon studies (UCLy); 1975 festival (BnF/ADCK); 1988 Matignon (BnF/UCLy; Paris negotiation context); 1989 assassination (BnF/UCLy; representative Ouvéa island pin). No precise killing-site coordinate is asserted.

**Rejected interpretation:** [Association Présence Kanak's biography](https://presencekanak.com/2020/09/22/jean-marie-tjibaou/) was directly inspected but excluded as a supporting data source: its introduction calls him a signatory of the 1998 Nouméa Accord, impossible for someone who died in 1989. Its description elsewhere of the 1988 Matignon negotiations does not cure that error. The new data deliberately distinguish Matignon, his death and the posthumous 1998 centre. Festival attendance totals and broad claims of universal Kanak representation are omitted. Tjibaou's political significance is not reduced to a consensus reconciliation story.

## V10 evidence follow-up: Samoa inquiry access resolved, review still bounded

The V10 ledger reports that the 1919 commission page returned only an iframe. On 24 October, its normal in-app browser UI exposed an **Image / Text / Research Info** tab group. Selecting **Text** made the OCR report readable:

[1919 Samoan Epidemic Commission, H-31c, Papers Past, Text tab](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/parliamentary/AJHR1919-I.2.2.4.46#text-tab).

| pp.9–10, extension of epidemic | 20 November Pago Pago message; criticism of failure to seek assistance; Logan's 28 November wireless-closure order and contradictory operator testimony | The commissioners discuss message ambiguity and Logan's interpretation; the new review does not flatten this into a simple direct refusal transcript. |

This resolves the **access limitation** for selected passages of `samoa-influenza-1918`; it does not certify all of the existing narrative, the complete inquiry evidence, translations, or modern epidemiological estimates. Relevant report OCR was read, not exhaustively collated against facsimile page images. Samoan time and New Zealand time differ in the source. No full report text is copied here.

## Remaining research

Still needed: community-authored Alaska follow-up on enforcement and household experience; original Jamaican broadcasts and reception; New Guinea local histories beyond high-office careers; regional or bilingual review of the French/English/Chinese Kanak synthesis; and Samoan-authored testimony alongside the colonial inquiry.
