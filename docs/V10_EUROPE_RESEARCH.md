# V10 Europe research and editorial decisions

Prepared 2026-10-23. This batch adds **16 bilingual records: eight biographies and eight connected histories**, with 35 dated biography milestones. It extends the existing `EU1`, `EU2` and `EU3` reading paths. The data file is [`expansion-europe-v10.json`](../dist/data/expansion-europe-v10.json); each source has a URL, publisher, claim-specific evidence note, access disclosure and review date.

## Scope and evidence

The sources were opened and their relevant text or catalogue descriptions inspected. A catalogue description is not a reading of the underlying manuscript. Multiple pages from one museum, archive or university remain one institutional source family. Original English and Chinese narratives use the same focus periods and material qualifications.

| Entry | Evidence used | Decision and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alexander | Metropolitan Museum curatorial essay and collection catalogue; War Museum Athens exhibition | Separate military chronology from later images and legendary anecdotes. Omit the erroneous age-at-death wording appearing on the museum pages. Selected river and regional pins do not establish a marching route. |
| Athenian assembly | Foundation of the Hellenic World institutional account | Describe adult male citizenship, exclusions and practical attendance barriers. The selected c.450–400 BCE interval is not an invented institutional lifespan. |
| Julius Caesar | Suetonius, *Julius* 19–20, 31–32, 40, 76, 82; Met republican history; Willamette coin catalogue | Distinguish retrospective literary testimony from contemporary coinage. Willamette reproduces a British Museum description and is not independent corroboration of it. Use a 59–44 BCE career window and an approximate Rubicon region. |
| Punic Wars | Met Italian chronology; Polybius, *Histories* I | Preserve the three separate intervals and their peace gaps. Omit the source timeline's questionable Saguntum date. Link Sicily to the existing Carthage record rather than centring the entire history on Rome. |
| Joan of Arc | International Joan of Arc Society chronology; British Library Stowe MS 84 catalogue; Royal Armouries | The 1456 annulment belongs to posthumous context, outside the 1429–1431 life milestones. Catalogue inspection is not a transcription of the trial manuscripts. |
| Hundred Years War | Royal Armouries and the Joan chronology/archive | State explicitly that 1337–1453 is a conventional enclosing span, not continuous fighting; acknowledge changing alliances. |
| Newton | St Andrews MacTutor biography and *Principia* preface extract; Oxford Newton Project corpus introduction | Distinguish 1642 Julian from 1643 Gregorian birth labels. Use the 1661–1727 career window. The corpus introduction supports the breadth of his writings, not a claim to have read every manuscript. |
| Du Châtelet | MacTutor biography; Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy | Treat *Institutions de physique* as philosophical work in its own right. Separate the 1749 death and completion context from the full translation's posthumous 1759 publication. |
| Encyclopédie | ARTFL project overview and editor's introduction | Recognize contributing authors and production work. Seventeen text volumes and eleven plate volumes belong to the original 1751–1772 sequence; later supplements are excluded. Both sources belong to one scholarly project. |
| Lovelace | Computer History Museum; Science Museum; 1843 text transcribed by York University | Explain the published notes and conditional symbolic applications. Qualify “first programmer”; do not claim the complete Analytical Engine existed during her life. |
| Shevchenko | Internet Encyclopedia of Ukraine; Shevchenko Museum Toronto | Connect poetry and visual art to serfdom, freedom, patronage and military exile. The 1847 capital pin marks interrogation, not an unsupported arrest location. The 1838 individual emancipation differs from the 1861 reform. |
| Russian emancipation | C. M. Clay's 1865 diplomatic report in *Foreign Relations of the United States*; accessible introduction to Michael Lynch's historical article | Situate the report's praise of the emperor as the writer's perspective. Do not reproduce its questionable exact decree date or generalize its acreage/population figures. Subscriber-only continuation was not inspected. |
| Women's parliamentary franchise | UK Parliament overview and Equal Franchise Act archive description | Distinguish partial eligibility in 1918 from equal voting qualifications at twenty-one in 1928. This is the UK parliamentary electorate, not a statement about every imperial jurisdiction. |
| Turing | King's College archive exhibition; GCHQ Turing and Pyry accounts | Recognize prior Polish work and collaboration. Keep the Bombe distinct from Colossus and a general-purpose computer. Use only the year 1954 for death, avoiding confusion with the date the body was found. |
| Bletchley network | GCHQ Pyry history and Jean Millar oral account | Place Millar's operational work at Eastcote, not automatically inside Bletchley Park. Distinguish mathematical analysis, machine operation, checking and maintenance. |
| First Fram expedition | Fram Museum expedition account | Separate the ship's drift from Nansen and Johansen's sledging journey. The source has inconsistent departure-day labels, so use year-level dates. Omit celebratory claims of untouched territory; add no route geometry. |

## Access gaps and excluded material

British Museum direct access, selected BnF pages, the Newton Institute biography, Bletchley Park pages and several Nansen biographical sources did not return usable full text. They were not silently promoted into inspected sources. A standalone Nansen biography was deferred; the expedition entry uses the accessible museum account. Alexander's legendary anecdotes and precise death circumstances were also excluded.

## Remaining research

This is a selected expansion, not parity with the China collection. Further work should deepen eastern and southeastern Europe, Baltic and Scandinavian social history, medieval urban and rural institutions, migration, labour and colonial connections. More original-language scholarship, translated primary texts and different perspectives would improve several entries presently anchored in institutional syntheses. No historical polygon, sovereignty claim or verified travel route is established by these biographies.
