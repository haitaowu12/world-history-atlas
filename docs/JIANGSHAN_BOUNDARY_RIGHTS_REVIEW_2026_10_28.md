# Jiangshan boundary rights and dating review — 28 October 2026

## Decision and scope

The current distribution no longer ships the older Jiangshan political and administrative polygons: 377 unique political features, 3,211 administrative features, and three separately published snapshot features. Forty-five geometry files were retired. The [machine-readable ledger](JIANGSHAN_BOUNDARY_RIGHTS_AUDIT_2026_10_28.json) records the original file hashes and every feature's identity, name, category and geometry hash without retaining coordinates. Phase metadata and separately attributed narratives, settlements, waterways and schematic corridors remain. This review does **not** establish individual provenance for those remaining non-polygon lines; they need a separate rights review.

[CHGIS V6](https://chgis.fas.harvard.edu/data/chgis/v6/) restricts redistribution. The [pinned later Jiangshan README](https://github.com/DeanChensj/jiangshan-map/blob/9799f0f61cff80922970d2a0b304e18527db7e1e/README.md#credits--license) expressly distinguishes Warring States territories and prefectures derived from CHGIS V6/Hartwell from its project license. The older extraction does not identify each feature's origin. We cannot infer that all of it was CHGIS, or that none of it was; a broad project license does not clear an unidentified third-party contribution. The current atlas therefore withholds this entire set pending feature-specific evidence or permission.

The removal covers the current application tree and the newly published Site version. It does **not** erase earlier Git commits, other branches or previously published Site versions; those remain a separate preservation and access question. This report does not provide a legal opinion or independently establish the rights in every upstream contribution.

## Replacement boundaries and chronology

- **c. 250 BCE, seven Warring States:** [Ian Remsen's map](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Warring_States_c._250_BC.svg) is described by its creator as own work dedicated to CC0 1.0. It names Natural Earth and cites *The Cambridge History of China*, volume 1, p. 39 for the historical outline; the book itself is not relicensed by the artist. Seven paths are extracted as separate approximate regions. The [Metropolitan Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/shang-and-zhou-dynasties-the-bronze-age-of-china) confirms the broad Warring States sequence and Qin unification, not the boundary vertices. The fixed c. 250 reference is displayed only for 250–231 BCE, with a warning that borders changed. Han's conquest in 230 is not represented by carrying the old seven-state map forward.
Six candidate features from [Historical Basemaps](https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps/tree/da7a4b735ecef70aebdc9c73e409d8a2500d50f3), dating to 400 and 300 BCE and 300, 400 and 500 CE, were initially selected for version 32. The publisher declares GPL-3.0, but does not identify each feature's original inputs. An independent cross-check highlighted that this is insufficient to demonstrate the same per-feature provenance required by this review. Those six candidates were consequently **withheld from the current version** pending further evidence. Their absence does not establish that the publisher violated the license or that their source was CHGIS. The broader existing Historical Basemaps collection elsewhere in the atlas was not part of this Jiangshan boundary audit and still requires its own source-lineage assessment.

Several tempting candidates were rejected: OpenHistoricalMap relations with modern border components or incomplete component-level license/source information; 1200 Southern Song and Jin geometries with visibly anachronistic northern extents; an overextended 1600 Ming candidate; and a 500 map's lingering Eastern Jin label. The atlas does not claim a complete substitute: in particular 403–251 BCE, most years from 230 BCE to 1885 CE, and the former 1200 and 1600 Chinese polity outlines remain incompletely mapped. Empty areas mean missing coverage, not an absence of people or political organization.

## Registration and reproducibility

The original SVG is retained in `references/china-open-boundaries/` and its SHA-256 is `736b1fe6313cfe8733f93c2c8e86471cc881583989a75da2502e3baecfadc304`. Its author specifies Albers equal-area projection (central meridian 110°, origin 30°, standard parallels 25° and 37°). The extraction samples curves and registers the stated projection using the Luoyang marker and 51 independent Natural Earth coastal vertices. The fitted median coast residual is approximately 1.24 image pixels (3.1 km); the Luoyang marker residual is approximately 5.5 pixels (14 km). These are registration checks, **not** estimates of historical frontier accuracy. Treat the registered positions as generalized, uncertain by at least roughly 15–20 km before the source map's own historical uncertainty. The Wei Daliang witness lies about 0.2 image pixels outside its colored edge and is within that registration uncertainty; it is not claimed as a strict point-in-polygon pass.

`python scripts/build-china-open-boundaries.py` requires the retained SVG. Its checked environment used Python with numpy 2.5.4, pyproj 3.8.0, shapely 2.2.0 and svgpathtools 1.8.0. The app itself has no Python package runtime dependency. The builder checks the SVG hash, normalizes polygon winding and emits one transport-sized layer; `map-snapshots.json` records their hashes, licenses and source-year membership. These tests establish reproducibility and renderability, not historical certainty.
