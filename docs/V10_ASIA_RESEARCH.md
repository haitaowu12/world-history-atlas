# V10 Asia research and editorial ledger

Research checked: **2026-10-23**. This ledger accompanies only `dist/data/expansion-asia-v10.json`.

## Delivered scope

This tranche contains **26 new connected records**: **9 South Asia, 7 non-China East Asia and 10 Southeast Asia**. **12 biographies** contain **44 substantive, dated milestones** (3–5 each). There are 3 ideas/institutional histories, 3 places, 4 cultural histories and 4 events. All records have original English and Chinese summaries, explicit date interpretation, representative point labels, useful facts and at least two valid related IDs. English summaries are 178–233 words; biographies are 202–233 words. The research supported this length without padding the biographies with unsupported early-life anecdotes.

There are **44 distinct cited URLs**. Relevant text was opened and inspected except the Philippine Commission on Women outcome statement, for which an official search-indexed passage was inspected after the body returned 403. That access limit is encoded in its source object and in the entry review limit. The primary suffrage enabling act was read directly. A scholarly Chiri Yukie PDF was eventually retrieved after its original journal URL failed; the final entry cites the retrieved PDF, not the earlier abstract-only fallback.

This is a source-supported editorial synthesis, **not independent specialist review**. Institutional commemorations are used for bounded chronology and collection evidence; their laudatory judgments are not reproduced as neutral historical findings. Linked primary documents establish what their authors stated or prescribed, not automatically the truth or implementation of every assertion. No territorial polygons or new polity timelines are introduced.

## Editorial and chronology decisions

- **Akbar:** 1556–1605 is the reign, not a fabricated lifespan. Conquest and negotiated service appear together with cultural patronage. The V&A page incorrectly identifies the *Razmnama* with the *Ramayana*; that equation is rejected. The page’s claim that a court chronicle is always historically accurate is also not adopted. Court coordinates are labelled as associated settings, not exact rooms, coronation sites or a reconstructed itinerary.
- **Mughal books:** the separate institutional record treats translators and book workers as agents. It does not duplicate the emperor’s biography or call every manuscript a personal work by Akbar. 1605 bounds the chosen reign, not the end of translation.
- **Tagore and Santiniketan:** the biography follows writing and educational institution-building. The companion record treats the school/university/rural-institute founding phase (1901, 1921, 1922). The institutions’ aspirations are distinguished from demonstrated equality or outcomes. Neither the later World Heritage inscription nor the end of the selected phase is treated as a foundation/closure date.
- **Savitribai Phule:** the inspected teaching and NCERT texts disagree on the exact March 1897 death date. The entry uses the year only. Different accounts of a foundling home and inflated first-ever claims were not used. Schooling, poetry, the accessible well and relief work supply the four milestones.
- **Ramanujan:** no unqualified first-Indian-FRS claim is taken from a college commemoration, and no retrospective diagnosis or conflicting exact death location is adopted. The 1918 Cambridge point denotes institutional affiliation, not physical attendance at the Royal Society election. Local supporters and work in Madras remain part of the narrative.
- **IACS:** the map anchors the original Bowbazar neighbourhood, not the later Jadavpur campus. 1928 is a research milestone, not institutional closure. Raman’s work explicitly includes K. S. Krishnan and the practical conditions of measurement.
- **Ceylon tea:** the Exeter account distinguishes kangani recruitment and debt from formal indenture. The entry does not homogenize all workers under one contract category. Its 1867–1949 window connects a commercial-planting reference to exclusion from citizenship/voting; it does not imply that Sri Lankan Tamil communities share one migration history or that their history ended in 1949.
- **Vaikom:** the 1925 settlement opened three approach roads; it did not establish full temple entry. Multiple organizers and external supporters are retained. A single inspected state institutional account supplies the bounded synthesis; no independent reconstruction from protest archives is claimed.
- **Tsuda:** study at Bryn Mawr in 1889–1892 is distinguished from the Tokyo school founded in 1900 and its 1905 certification permission. The original urban location is not confused with the later Kodaira campus. No claim to the first female education in Japan is made.
- **Chiri Yukie:** the record spans her 1903–1922 life; the posthumous 1923 book is discussed in prose, outside the life milestones. The retrieved scholarly introduction confirms the 1909 Asahikawa household and 1922 Tokyo editing. Its impossible vocational-school graduation year is rejected. The oral knowledge of Imekanu and Monashinouku, her authorship as translator, and continued Ainu culture prevent a disappearing-people narrative. The scholarship was read in English; no Ainu philological verification is claimed.
- **Kim Gu:** 1931 organization and 1932 attacks are described as violent tactics, without presenting all independence activity as nonviolent. The 1948 Pyongyang talks are included; no claim about the ultimate direction of the assassination plot is made. The museum’s contested description of the 1896 killing victim and its speculation about unification were not used.
- **Korean provisional institutions:** 1919–1945 is the exile phase, ending with return, not a ruling-territory interval or an assertion that every legal continuity ceased in 1945. Multiple initiatives and fundraising are included. Shanghai is an exile institutional setting, not a Korean sovereignty polygon in China; `kind: idea` avoids conflating representation with territorial control.
- **Park Du-seong:** 1913 teaching, 1923 student collaboration and 1926 six-dot promulgation are the three milestones. Four-dot tactile teaching had precedents. The later 1946 manuscript is discussed without inventing its writing location. The National Archives report and museum account are related Korean heritage institutions, not two independent research biographies.
- **Tomioka:** 1872–1893 frames the government model mill, not its full operation. Women trainees are agents in skills transmission; this showcase factory is not generalized to every Japanese mill’s working conditions.
- **Seowon:** 1550–1650 is explicitly a rounded window for the nine heritage academies’ mid-sixteenth to mid-seventeenth-century founding phase. It is not an exact foundation date for the representative Sosu site or all Korean schools. Elite educational institutions are not presented as universal access.
- **Dewi Sartika:** the inspected provincial culture article supports school changes in 1904, 1910 and 1911 and displacement after 1945. Its inconsistent marriage-age arithmetic and obvious typographical year are omitted. The 1980 government monograph was located but timed out in retrieval; it is not passed off as inspected corroboration. Geography after departure is not plotted as an exact itinerary.
- **Ho Chi Minh:** a museum exhibition supplies the 1911 departure, the Australian collection record supplies broad career chronology, and a contemporary US diplomatic report supplies the 1946 appeal/negotiation episode. The latter proves an argument was recorded, not every claim in the petition. Collective leadership is acknowledged. The Australian page’s compressed Saigon-renaming chronology is omitted. His death precedes the later unification; he is not assigned later actions.
- **Nguyễn Thị Bình:** 1968–2002 is a public-career focus, not a closed lifespan. The Southern Women’s Museum’s two signing pens establish material provenance and a documented act; they do not prove that signing settled every conflict. A ministry newspaper’s first-hand exhibition report supplies 1976–1987 and 1992–2002 office dates. Both sources belong to related commemorative contexts; they are not independent evaluations of diplomatic effectiveness. The disputed birth location is omitted.
- **Josefa Llanes Escoda:** institutional sources disagree on birth year, so 1919–1945 is a career window. The NHCP article also has a problematic suffrage-act number, which is not repeated. The Girl Scouts charter is dated from the NCCA account, separately from organizational activity in preceding years. The January 1945 last sighting is not converted into a proven execution date or grave coordinate.
- **Ifugao:** the UNESCO page retains an older unchanged-two-thousand-year story; the research team’s radiocarbon/archaeobotanical interpretation places major wet-rice intensification around the seventeenth century. The disagreement is visible in both languages. 1600–1900 is an interpretive window, not a universal construction interval or first highland occupation. No new boundary geometry is claimed.
- **Ban Chiang:** 2100 BCE–200 CE follows the explicitly named Ban Chiang Project ceramic chronology. Penn and ISEAA share that research programme and are not independent chronological confirmation. No earliest-metallurgy or first-civilization ranking is made, and regional dates are not presented as settled for all purposes. A comparative review of competing dating models remains desirable.
- **Plain of Jars:** sampled Site 2 jar emplacement (OSL estimate 1240–660 BCE) is separated from ninth–thirteenth-century CE mortuary dates on other materials. The broad display range is not evidence of uninterrupted activity by one culture, and it does not date every jar. UNESCO’s older 500 BCE–500 CE model is reported as a different framework rather than silently combined. The point is regional, not an invented sample coordinate.
- **Philippine suffrage:** the primary 1936 act specifies a 1937 plebiscite, qualification rules, 300,000 affirmative votes and women’s-club observation. The act does not establish the certified result; the official outcome statement is separately identified as indexed-only after a 403. No exact vote tally is asserted. Voting remained subject to qualifications, and the Manila anchor does not imply a city-only election.
- **Malolos:** the 1940 marker is a later commemoration, explicitly distinguished from full contemporary proceedings and the constitution’s individual clauses. The entry is an assembly/location history, not a duplicate republic polity or a claim that constitutional text secured effective rule everywhere. A full article-by-article constitutional analysis is held for later research.
- **Youth Pledge:** the 1928 congress and common political language are distinct from independent statehood or the disappearance of other languages. The museum’s archival interpretation describes an instrumental violin presentation of *Indonesia Raya*, not a first full vocal performance. The original archival files cited by the museum were not independently inspected.

## Deduplication and connections

Before selecting candidates, the current core and V9 Asia shards were inspected, including `expansion-south-west-asia-v9.json` and `expansion-east-southeast-v9.json`. New biographies add different people; Kartini, Rizal, Rokeya, Pandita Ramabai, Ambedkar, Murasaki, Yosano Akiko, Yu Gwan-sun and existing major independence records are linked rather than recreated. Akbar’s biography and the translation institution add distinct lenses to the existing Mughal polity and Fatehpur Sikri place. Chiri’s authored life complements the broader existing Ainu continuity record. Kim Gu and the exile institution complement the existing March First movement and Korean division records. Tomioka is a labour/technology institution, not another Meiji-restoration event. Malolos concerns the assembly, rather than duplicating the 1898 declaration or Philippine–American War.

A scan of all current `dist/data/*.json` files found **zero exact ID collisions**, **zero missing related IDs**, **zero milestones outside their record intervals**, and no empty English/Chinese summary or source array. Every biography has 3–5 milestones; all milestone `approximate` flags are false because the plotted years are sourced years. Representative/uncertain geography is instead disclosed in labels and bilingual date notes. The arithmetic check used the on-disk files available at integration time; the root project performs the full load/build/test gates.

Related links are reading links, including comparisons, not claims that every linked person met or directly influenced another. In particular, Ramanujan/IACS links compare paths of science, and the Ifugao/Bali and Escoda/Singapore links compare community/occupation histories. No edge or course should be interpreted as a verified travel route.

## Held proposals and remaining research

Sugihara was considered but deferred because his decisive documented activity would chiefly extend the European refugee story rather than deepen the selected Japanese institutional contexts. A separate Korean-braille invention event and standalone school founding entries for every new educator were avoided because they would largely duplicate the biographies. Additional regional people should be developed from local archival/scholarly sources rather than by converting generic famous-name lists into markers.

Further useful work includes independent archival corroboration for Dewi Sartika and Vaikom; competing Ban Chiang chronology review; complete Malolos proceedings and constitutional articles; historical rather than celebratory assessments of Nguyễn Thị Bình’s education policies; multilingual student/worker testimony for the new education and industrial entries; and community-reviewed Ainu/Ifugao interpretation. These are held research tasks, not claims that the current limited syntheses have completed them.
