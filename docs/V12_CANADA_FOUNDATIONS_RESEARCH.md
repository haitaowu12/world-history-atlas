# V12 Canada foundations: lives, knowledge and public institutions

Research review date: **24 October 2026**. Accompanies only `dist/data/expansion-canada-foundations-v12.json`.

## Scope and inspection method

Eight new bilingual records form four biography/context pairs: Samuel de Champlain and map-making; John A. Macdonald and the National Policy’s economic/administrative mechanisms; Tommy Douglas and Saskatchewan insurance; Terry Fox and the organization of public fundraising. **Four biographies contain nineteen dated milestones**. English biographies contain 262–277 words; contexts contain 198–209 words. Each has `countryGroup: canada`, existing `NA2`/`NA3` workstreams, at least two related IDs, a bilingual review limit, and explicit date/location interpretation. No polity bars, route legs or territorial geometry are added by these files.

## Conflicts and decisions affecting the text

1. **Champlain birth:** C02 gives approximately 1567; C01 describes several disputed possibilities and treats a 1574 baptismal identification as unproven. The biography therefore declares a 1603–1635 career focus and supplies no invented birth milestone. The late-July 1609 battle day also differs between descriptions; only the year is used in prose. C02’s adjacent 1628/1629 seizure statements are compressed; the adopted Québec seizure year is 1629 from C01.
2. **Vignau and Tessouat:** C01 says the exact truth of the northern-sea report is lost. The context does not call the interpreter conclusively fraudulent or cast Tessouat’s refusal as mere obstruction; trade/portage control and Indigenous decision-making remain visible. No source’s assertion that Champlain’s map contained everything known about North America is repeated.
3. **Macdonald dates/responsibility:** registered birthday 10 January and family celebration 11 January do not need forced reconciliation; only 1815 is used. His two premierships are separated. In particular, he was in opposition when the Indian Act passed in 1876. His later administration’s use and reinforcement of coercive systems is described through the relevant 1878–1887 tenure and 1883 school expansion. The 1885 head-tax law and full effect in 1886 are not conflated. The text does not attribute all later federal actions to one individual.
4. **Competing moral/legal appraisals:** C03’s late analytical sections offer a contested assessment of genocide terminology. This contribution neither treats that as settled adjudication nor rewrites the existing NCTR/TRC-based school entry. It uses the specific documented mechanisms—food control, reserve coercion, assimilationist schooling and racial exclusion—rather than assigning an unsupported new legal conclusion. Policy intentions and harms remain explicit in English and Chinese.
5. **Douglas biographies:** C05 dates family immigration to 1911, C06 to 1910; C05 gives 1930 ordination/move to Weyburn, C06 1929 full-time ministry; C05 gives NDP leadership end in 1971, C06 in 1972. The biographies can support a meaningful story without plotting those disputed dates. Both support the main election/premiership/insurance chronology. Douglas’s 1933 eugenic ideas are not hidden by a purely celebratory life story.
6. **Medicare stages:** hospital insurance began in 1947. The physician-service bill became law in November 1961 after Douglas’s departure, took effect 1 July 1962 under Woodrow Lloyd, and was followed by the 23 July agreement ending the strike. A short account cannot credit Douglas with personally implementing every stage. The historical museum Medicare URL supplied as a lead was removed; its captured replacement/landing text is not used as history evidence.
7. **Fox fundraising chronology:** C08’s narrative places $24.17 million near the September 1980 stopping episode, while C09 explicitly dates reaching that goal to 1 February 1981 and describes the September telethon separately. The new entries use the dated sequence and largely omit exact sums. C08’s page also contains $850-million and $1-billion cumulative wording, while C09 is a 2020 reference with older cumulative figures. None is presented as the current verified total. The original 1980 run, later fundraising and annual run beginning in 1981 are distinguished.
8. **Locations and map scope:** contemporary locality coordinates orient readers to historical episodes. They are not reconstructed frontier polygons, exact route traces, verified hospital beds, specific parliamentary rooms or the precise spot of Fox’s final stride. Selecting these entries must not silently imply that a colonial map or 1867 federation already encompassed modern Canadian territory.

## Continuation priorities

The Canada material remains selective. Stronger next work would add Indigenous-authored accounts around Champlain’s alliances and cartographic information; First Nations and Chinese community sources alongside official federal policy history; Saskatchewan patients, clinic members and doctors’ contemporary records; and local donor/volunteer evidence beyond a national commemorative campaign. Those additions can deepen the same sequences without duplicating national turning-point entries.

This file contributes narrative records only. Main integration owns the separate North American map-detail work, the generated content manifest/reading paths and browser acceptance. No new geometry, treaty interpretation or route connection is established by this research.
