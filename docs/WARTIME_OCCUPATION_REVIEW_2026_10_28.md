# Wartime context and map display review · 28 October 2026

## Outcome and limits

The atlas has a selective, bilingual occupation and liberation layer for the two world wars and selected postwar occupations. It is **not** a complete war map. Country hatching reuses the existing CShapes 2.0 year-end, de jure country-reference unit. It expresses a documented broad occupation, not the borders between occupiers, exact areas of military control, a daily front, or continuous uniform governance. Greece's single hatch covers an explicitly divided occupation; Denmark's European reference excludes the separately governed Faroes and Greenland. CShapes' western German zone combines western areas; Berlin's four-power administration is not separately reconstructed. All CShapes geometry retains its CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 attribution and original coordinates.

Diamonds refer to documented places or represent countries with partial or changing control. They are not boundaries. Entries for 1944 and 1945 explicitly identify in-year changes and do not imply that every place remained occupied at year-end. The overview presents the scope and source links, and each selected country or marker opens its supporting history. At world scale, diamonds do not intercept pointer clicks on small countries; zoom in or use the overview to select them. The layer can be disabled and is absent from Modern geography. Coverage excludes many occupied areas, including precise partitions of Poland, France and Czechoslovakia, WWI fronts, and most of the Soviet Union and Southeast Asia. These must not be inferred from unmarked areas.

No new occupation polygons or external geometry were imported. The Stanford/ArcGIS teaching layer examined during research encodes selected annexations and political entities, not comprehensive occupation, and has separate use restrictions. It was not used to claim occupation boundaries. Narratives do not authorize a new frontier. Specialist, regional, community and bilingual review remain outstanding.
