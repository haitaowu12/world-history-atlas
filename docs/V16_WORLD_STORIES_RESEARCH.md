# V16: histories through eight objects

Review date: 9 November 2026. Additive input: `dist/data/expansion-world-stories-v16.json`, editorial release 10, edition `v16`.

## Editorial scope

This batch connects eight surviving objects or documented object groups to existing histories of work, exchange, knowledge and family life. It adds two stories each in Africa, West/Central Asia, Latin America and Oceania. These are bounded object studies, not comprehensive regional overviews or new territorial reconstructions. There are no new full biographies, quotations, invented conversations or reconstructed journeys.

The existing base and manifest-listed V15 corpus were searched by subject and title before writing. Existing entries already cover, among other subjects, Deir el-Medina, Akan/Asante politics, Inka recordkeeping, Maya societies, Pacific wayfinding and Tongan institutions. The new entries therefore focus on different evidence-bearing objects and connect to those stable IDs. The illustrated Inka tunic is not the Caleta Vitor tunic used for comparison. Related Carolinian and Marshallese readings do not establish a common technical tradition.

Every source listed below was opened and its relevant passage inspected. `checkedAt` is `2026-11-09`; each record has English and Chinese evidence notes and a bilingual review limit. Summaries are original paraphrases. Source authority does not remove uncertainty, including uncertainty expressed by the source itself.

## Source conflicts and material excluded

- **Nanni:** the CDLI catalogue appears to label centimetre-scale object measurements in millimetres. Dimensions are unnecessary here and were omitted. The British Museum collection endpoint returned 403; it is not marked as inspected. The accessible scholarly manuscript supplies the translation and the photographed date label. The university student blog was inspected during discovery but is not the evidential basis for the narrative.
- **Babylonian recipes:** the specialist catalogue's Old Babylonian range is wider than dates frequently repeated in popular accounts. The wider range is retained. The distinction between three Old Babylonian recipe tablets and a larger culinary collection is not collapsed into an unsupported total.
- **Akan weights:** the Met and Penn State discussions differ in how confidently they assign practical weighing use to figurative objects. The text preserves the Met's caution. The Met page's unrelated claim about eighteenth-century British conquest was not reused. No meaning is invented for an undeciphered pattern.
- **Maya vessel:** chemical evidence from other vessels would not establish the contents of this one. No residue test is asserted. Its probable source region and the curator's handling interpretation remain qualified.
- **Inka tunic:** the original workshop and wearer are unknown. A separate excavated garment can support comparison about imperial and local production, not a provenance claim for the Met object.
- **Marshallese charts:** the Library of Congress article's very broad claim of a millennia-old origin is not used. The story focuses on dated objects and documented teaching. Charts are not recreated as exact navigational maps.
- **Ngatu:** Te Papa's descriptive paragraphs refer both to a kupesi rubbing board and, more specifically, a coarse pandanus mat. That inconsistency is recorded in the bilingual review limit; no specific rubbing surface is asserted. Another royal cloth considered during research had conflicting 1953 and 1955 dates and was not selected.
