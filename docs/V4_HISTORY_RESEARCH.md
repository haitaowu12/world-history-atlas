## Research method and interpretation

Sources were selected from national archives, legislatures, treaty repositories, museums, archaeological missions, universities, UN institutions and the Polynesian Voyaging Society. Original documents and objects are used where available: constitutions, proclamations, legislation, treaties, petitions, archival photographs, cuneiform tablets and navigation testimony. Institutional historical explanations supply context when an original document alone cannot establish causes or consequences. An official source is not assumed to be neutral: government promises, colonial territorial claims and royal self-presentation are identified as claims or commitments.

Each source has an evidenceNote identifying the specific support it supplies. Sources were inspected through accessible full pages, archival catalog descriptions and indexed institutional text. Some legacy archive pages and PDFs could not be fetched in full by the research browser; their indexed descriptions support the limited claims cited. This is not a claim that every archival page or every page of a multi-page treaty volume was exhaustively reviewed, nor a permanent link-availability audit.

English narratives and Chinese translations are original editorial syntheses. They avoid quotations and fabricated precision. All new intervals are finite and ongoing is false: a foundation, publication or independence event is not silently kept active through 2026. A location pin identifies a relevant place, not a country's extent. Related-reading links are thematic or sequential, and do not imply that connected events happened in the same year.

## Important date and category decisions

| Case | Decision and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Kerma | Use rounded c. 2500–1500 BCE and disclose the mission's more specific c. 2450/1480 phase boundaries. Ancient archaeological chronologies differ; the record is not an exact annual territorial claim. |
| Ur III | Use conventional 2112–2004 BCE and explicitly mark absolute chronology as approximate. The Umma ration tablet and the Ur capital marker are different locations. |
| Babylon | Separate the Neo-Babylonian dynasty from Hammurabi's earlier kingdom and the much longer occupation of the city. |
| Baghdad, 1258 | End Abbasid rule from Baghdad, not all later Abbasid titles or intellectual activity throughout the Islamic world. No speculative death toll or legend about ink-black rivers is included. |
| Westphalia | Explain imperial estates' limited external rights, rather than presenting a simplified 'birth of modern sovereignty' claim. Distinguish Julian and Gregorian dates. |
| Vienna | The Final Act of 9 June 1815 preceded Waterloo on 18 June; it was not negotiated only after that battle. |
| Italy | Use Turin for 1861. Venetia's 1866 incorporation and Rome's 1870 incorporation are later stages. |
| Poland | Treat 11 November 1918 as a commemorative institutional milestone, not the date all borders became settled. |
| India | Separate Company power, the 1858 transfer to Crown government, 1947 independence/partition, 1949 constitutional adoption and 1950 general commencement. Article 394 provides early commencement for specified provisions. |
| Ceylon and Fiji | Explain independent dominion status and retain contemporary names rather than turning independence automatically into a republic. |
| Korea | Distinguish the 1945 occupation boundary at the 38th parallel, separate states in 1948, the outbreak in 1950 and the negotiated 1953 military demarcation line. The armistice was not a peace treaty. |
| Shimonoseki | The original Liaodong cession was reversed through subsequent diplomacy; the first treaty map is not treated as permanent. |
| Malaya / Malaysia | Keep 1957 Malayan independence separate from Malaysia's 1963 formation and Singapore's 1965 separation. |
| Philippines | Keep the 1898 declaration separate from 1946 independence from the United States; distinguish treaty signature from entry into force. |
| Louisiana and Alaska | Treat transactions as transfers between states over populated Indigenous lands, without equating those contracts with Indigenous consent. Alaska's treaty, transfer and payment have different dates. |
| Great Peace of Montréal | Sources count participating nations differently, including 38 and 39. Use 'dozens' and foreground the treaty and Indigenous diplomacy instead of pretending the count is exact. |
| Gran Colombia | Use 1819 proclamation, 1821 constitution and 1830–1831 dissolution as separate stages. Include the final 1831 New Granadan institutional transition in the endpoint. |
| Brazil | Independence, imperial acclamation, Portuguese recognition and abolition of slavery are distinct events. |
| Panama | Independence in 1903 did not equal full practical control of the canal zone; connect with the existing 1999 canal-transfer record. |
| Hawaiʻi | Separate 1810 consolidation, 1893 overthrow, 1898 annexation and the 1993 apology. Kauaʻi entered through acknowledgement of supremacy, not successful invasion in 1810. |
| Hōkūleʻa | Describe a 1976 voyage grounded in living Micronesian expertise. Do not claim Pacific navigation had vanished everywhere or that this voyage alone proves every ancient migration. |
| Nunavut | Classify 1999 as internal territorial creation within Canada; distinguish public territorial institutions from Inuit land-claims rights. |

## Guided reading courses

### From empires to new political orders / 从帝国到新的政治秩序

Follow three connected histories: Abbasid and Ottoman rule, Company and Crown government in India, and the transitions from Qing rule. Compare conquest, transfer of government, revolution and constitutional adoption. Each change has its own date and evidence.

ID: course-imperial-successions. Suggested reading time: 35 minutes. 13 linked records.

Sequence: abbasid-caliphate → baghdad-foundation → baghdad-sack-1258 → ottoman-empire → turkish-republic-proclamation → mughal-empire → battle-plassey → crown-rule-india → south-asia-independence-partition → india-constitution → qing-dynasty → xinhai-revolution → prc-proclamation.

Activity: Choose one of the three sequences. Build a four-column evidence table: event, effective date, governing institution and what remains uncertain. Use at least one primary document from a record's sources. Then write a two-sentence map caption that states the observation year and avoids claiming more territorial precision than the evidence supports.

### How records, routes and skills connect worlds / 记录、通道与技艺如何连接世界

Move from Mesopotamian accounts and Korean script design to steam technology, canals, railways and Pacific navigation. Compare the practical knowledge behind a connection with the institutions and labour that make it work.

ID: course-records-routes-knowledge. Suggested reading time: 30 minutes. 11 linked records.

Sequence: uruk-writing → ur-third-dynasty → baghdad-foundation → person-al-khwarizmi → hangul-promulgation → watt-separate-condenser → straits-settlements-formation → suez-canal-opening → transcontinental-railroad → person-tupaia → hokulea-first-voyage.

Activity: Pick one written record and one transport or navigation case. For each, list the skill, material, people and institution required. Cite a specific object or document from the source list. Finish with a comparison explaining one benefit and one limitation of treating a connection as a simple line on a map.

### Many paths to independence and self-government / 独立与自治的多种道路

Compare independence, federation, union and territorial self-government across Latin America, Africa, Asia, the Arctic and the Pacific. Read founding documents alongside the institutions that followed, and separate changing statehood from the rights of every person within it.

ID: course-paths-self-government. Suggested reading time: 35 minutes. 12 linked records.

Sequence: paraguay-independence → brazil-independence → gran-colombia → ceylon-independence → burma-independence → malaya-independence → nigeria-independence → kenya-independence → tanganyika-zanzibar-union → fiji-independence → papua-new-guinea-independence → nunavut-creation.

Activity: Select three cases from different regions. Make a comparison with the legal act, its date, the new institution and a question it did not settle. Classify each as independence, constitutional change, union or internal territorial creation. Explain your classification using the linked documents rather than the shape or colour of a map.

Course questions and activities are editorial teaching prompts, not objective quizzes. Their historical premises are supported by the linked records; they do not introduce unsourced factual mini-lectures.

## Source inventory and claim support

The entries below identify every source used by the 46 additions. 'Primary' is not used as a blanket label: a digitized original is a primary source, while a modern museum or government historical explanation is an institutional secondary account.

### Kerma: a Nubian kingdom on the Nile

ID: kerma-kingdom · Region: africa · Years: -2500 to -1500 · Location year: -1750.

Approximately 2500–1500 BCE is a rounded archaeological span. The mission's phase table places Early Kerma around 2450 BCE and Egyptian conquest around 1480 BCE; other site descriptions use slightly different approximate endpoints.

- [Kerma: history and archaeological chronology](https://kerma.ch/en/history/) — Swiss archaeological mission at Kerma. Capital location, cultural phases, monuments and the approximate transition to Egyptian occupation.
- [Beaker, Classic Kerma, found at Abydos](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545772) — The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Catalogued Nubian pottery from an Egyptian archaeological context provides object-level evidence of contact.

Related reading: memphis-pyramids, egypt-new-kingdom, meroe-kush.

### Ur III: governing through clay records

ID: ur-third-dynasty · Region: west-central-asia · Years: -2112 to -2004 · Location year: -2100.

2112–2004 BCE follows a conventional ancient Near Eastern chronology; absolute dates are approximate. The cited messenger-ration tablet is dated by its museum to about 2028 BCE and comes from Umma, not Ur.

- [Cuneiform tablet: rations for messengers](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/327069) — The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Primary administrative object from Umma, approximately 2028 BCE, recording specified rations.
- [Foundation figure of Ur-Namma](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/329067) — The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Primary royal foundation object and the conventional reign dates of Ur-Namma.
- [Cuneiform tablet: receipt of metals](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/324589) — The Metropolitan Museum of Art. Collection record supplies the conventional 2112–2004 BCE Ur III period.

Related reading: uruk-writing, akkadian-empire, old-babylon.

## Validation and remaining scope

The builder checks unique new IDs, date order, finite endpoints, location-year containment, two fact pairs, at least two references, and all related/course record references. It also checks that retained existing objects remain unchanged after serialization. The project's record validator is run separately on the complete history-plus-people collection.

Completed validation returned zero record errors for all 264 history and biography records. All related-reading and course references resolve. A comparison with Git HEAD confirmed that every one of the 194 pre-existing history objects and all nine pre-existing course objects were preserved without changes. Each new English summary has 83–97 words; its Chinese counterpart has 144–192 characters.

This is a substantial curated expansion, not an exhaustive history of all societies. The records explain transitions and provide source trails; they do not certify the accuracy of polygon borders. Detailed frontier reconstruction, contested modern status and daily territorial control still require their own dated cartographic sources. No political entity is extended to 2026 merely because a record was researched in 2026.
