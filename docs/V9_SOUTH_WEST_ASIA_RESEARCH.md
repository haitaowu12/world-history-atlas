## Date, identity and perspective decisions

- Political focus intervals describe phases. Vijayanagara’s 1336–1565 capital-centered phase does not end all successor rule; Maratha 1818 ends Peshwa power, not every Maratha state; Mysore 1761–1799 isolates Hyder/Tipu rule. Bahmani 1527 and the Met’s 1538 convention are both disclosed. Golconda’s formal 1518 convention is distinguished from earlier autonomy.
- Chagatai ends at the selected early phase, not every later Chagataid line. Bukhara combines changing ruling houses and distinguishes the Russian protectorate. Khiva distinguishes changing capitals, the 1873 protectorate and 1920 regime change. Kazakh 1731 concerns a partial imperial relationship, not the end of all khanates or a people. Pastoralism is not equated with a uniform, stateless or isolated society.
- The 1924–1929 delimitation record distinguishes Tajik autonomy within Uzbekistan from the 1929 union republic; Soviet republic boundaries are not described as international independence. The Aral interval is an analytical window, without invented annual shorelines.
- Aryabhata’s 499 text anchors an inferred 476 birth; uncertain birthplace does not justify claiming that he headed Nalanda. Mirabai’s traditional 1546/1547 death conventions and attribution/oral-transmission limits are explicit. Enheduanna’s dates are a broad scholarly focus; the object supports a named priestly identity, not the precise date of every attributed poem.
- NLM records Hunayn’s death as 873 or 877; 873 is a conventional approximate endpoint, and 850 is a rounded activity marker. Al-Biruni’s conventional 1048 endpoint is qualified. Tahirih’s birth conventions vary; 1817 is explicitly a convention. Soraya uses a 1919–1929 public-role focus to avoid choosing among discrepant birth years. Nazik is situated among several Arabic free-verse contributors, not named the sole inventor; 1947 is a publication-era anchor.
- Constitutional texts establish wording and adoption contexts; they do not prove equal implementation, continuous parliamentary operation or universal consent. The Ottoman constitution is not described as continuously operative after 1876. The Maldives record distinguishes 1965 independence and 1968 republic. Nepal’s 2015 constitution is not equated with the 2006 peace settlement. Pakistan and Bangladesh entries do not update current officeholders or present-day legal text.
- The Indus entry concerns the 1960 treaty, not its current operational status. A secondary description calling the World Bank a guarantor was not adopted; the text describes its limited procedural role. Green Revolution production gains are not generalized to every farmer or region. Chipko is not reduced to one undifferentiated women’s movement or a single date.
- Israel/Palestine separates the 1947 UN partition proposal, 1948 declaration and war, Palestinian displacement remembered as the Nakba, and 1949 armistice lines. Proposal is not control, and armistice is not a permanent final border. The source set combines UN and US institutional perspectives but remains indexed-only because the opened pages failed to supply usable body text. It is a bounded introduction, not a settlement of historical or present legal disputes.
- Taif’s 1989 agreement is distinguished from 1990 transition and uneven implementation. The Refworld PDF and Lebanese Mission edition are copies of the same primary text. Saudi Arabia’s September 1932 decree/name change is distinguished from commemorative observance. Iran–Iraq invasion/war and August 1988 ceasefire are separate from later monitoring.
- US diplomatic accounts, British military museums, royal collections and official state histories have situated perspectives. Colonial objects and archives are discussed with their histories of conquest and removal. Security Council Resolution 1483’s treatment of occupation is not retroactive universal authorization of the 2003 invasion. Soviet withdrawal in 1989 is not an end to Afghan war or proof all refugees returned.
- No modern constitutional history is projected onto ancient geography. No religious, linguistic or archaeological tradition is reclassified as a uniform state merely to gain a timeline row. Source stereotypes or sweeping cultural generalizations are not reproduced as conclusions.
