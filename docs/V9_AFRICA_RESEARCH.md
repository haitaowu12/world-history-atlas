# V9 Africa expansion: evidence and limits

Authoring/research date: 23 October2026. Baseline: V8. Output: `dist/data/expansion-africa-v9.json`.

## Delivered scope

48 original bilingual entries:36 histories and12 biographies;6,919 English summary words and more than12,000 Chinese characters. The data has15 political overviews,9 events,6 culture entries,5 place histories,1 idea/policy history and12 people. Only political entries carry timeline metadata; two are secondary. No boundary geometry or asserted itinerary has been added.

Njinga belongs to the Angola/central-coast comparative work in AF3; Njoya belongs to Cameroon/cultural-history work in AF3. Neither has been reclassified into the Great Lakes/Congo core simply to meet a numerical target.

## Evidence standard and actual retrieval

This is a **mixed-access synthesis**. Institutional essays, heritage descriptions, selected primary documents, archival/museum accounts and scholarly abstracts/extracts were compared. The source register below distinguishes inspected text from indexed descriptions, catalogue-only records, unextractable PDFs and primary-document snippets. Successful identification of a URL is not full-text review. This batch must not be described as an independently corroborated claim-by-claim edition.

83 source URLs were initially used; the final checks add a Bunyoro conquest chapter, an archival historian’s women-history account and a Tanzanian tentative-list description. Most records have at least two relevant URLs, but these are not always independent evidence families. Kasubi, Harar and Khami rely on one detailed UNESCO site assessment; the Burundi performance record uses a UNESCO nomination and the related committee decision. The Middle Kingdom/Napata records use two Met contributions from the same institution. These limitations are intentional and should not be hidden by treating URL counts as confidence.

Catalogue-only UNESCO chapters on the Great Lakes and Luba/Lunda are research leads, not proof of precise founding dates. Broad century boundaries in those records are declared focus windows. Archaeological cultural material, tributary relations and sacred ancestry are not equated with direct territorial rule.

English and Chinese narratives are original companion syntheses with matching claims. UNESCO’s Chinese Kasubi description was consulted as a terminology comparison; no source paragraphs or historical images were copied. Most other evidence is English, with French institutional biography material for Senghor.

## Decisions made during review

- Berlin1884–85 is a framework for imperial claims, not the conference at which all African borders were drawn. The German archive’s explicit correction takes precedence over older museum-chronology shorthand.
- Luba/Lunda sacred founders and Ethiopian Solomonic ancestry are described as legitimating traditions, not verified genealogies.
- Rwanda’s changing social categories are not treated as timeless races or as an inevitable cause of1994 genocide. Genocide, civil war and other violence remain analytically distinct.
- Kasubi’s1882 palace/1884 burial conversion is separate from the continuing site and its later reconstruction.
- Njoya the ruler and Prince Ibrahim Njoya the artist are kept distinct. The Met’s c.1892–1933 cultural focus does not settle conflicting accession dates.
- Nana Asma’u follows1793–1864 in the SOAS main text and Leiden entry; the inconsistent1865 image caption was rejected.
- Njinga’s resistance does not imply that she opposed all slave trading or operated outside its economy.
- Zanzibar’s1873 prohibition is not the immediate end of slavery; the Tanzanian submission explicitly notes clandestine continuation.
- The ujamaa record distinguishes voluntary1967–72 villagization from compulsory Operation Vijiji after1973, following an inspected scholarly note.
- The EAC biography’s unsupported “first voluntary retiree” and continent-wide agricultural ranking claims were excluded from Nyerere’s biography.
- Death totals for colonial genocides, Maji Maji, Biafra and Rwanda are not presented as exact statistics.
- Gbowee’s2003–2011 and Njinga’s1624–1663 records are explicit career focus periods, not asserted lifespans.
