# Chinese source descriptions — 28 October 2026

The Chinese interface now displays translated source descriptions in story details, the source catalog, map evidence, routes, and the people and places directories. English source descriptions remain unchanged and are available from each translation's disclosure. English mode continues to show the original descriptions. Source titles and publisher names remain in their original language.

The translation overlay is separate from the published V14 narrative data and map geometry. The inventory covers 1,783 distinct `evidenceNote` strings across 7,512 data occurrences, plus descriptions constructed at runtime. The manifest records the shard sizes and hashes. Chinese shards are loaded on demand, only for a Chinese view; the atlas does not call a translation service at runtime. If the overlay cannot be loaded or a future source lacks a translation, the original is shown with an explicit missing-translation label.

The descriptions were generated with Apple's English-to-Simplified-Chinese translation model. The final overlay has 1,791 translations across four shards totaling 518,748 bytes, including 119 editorial overrides. Selected high-frequency geometry notes, runtime notes, recent early-faith, cross-regional, Indus and Indigenous additions, and the Atlantic route were edited against the English originals, as were specific mistakes found during checks. The interface identifies the overlay as machine-assisted and offers the original English for inspection. It is **not** a claim that every translation has received specialist, community, or bilingual editorial acceptance; subtle errors and awkward wording may remain.

`scripts/audit-source-notes-zh.mjs` flags changed numerical details, potentially lost negation, and potentially lost BCE markers for manual review. The assembled overlay produced no remaining flags after the identified problems were corrected. This is a triage aid, not a semantic proof: a matched number does not prove that it refers to the correct event, and negation may still be mistranslated. The focused religious-history checks preserve, among other limits, the contested Lumbini birth date, 313 CE policy versus implementation, and Sikh community perspective.

For new source descriptions, add a Chinese translation to `scripts/source-notes-zh-reviewed.mjs` and review dates, negation, uncertainty, and source-access limitations against the original. Run `node scripts/build-source-notes-zh.mjs --from-shards` to merge the additions with the committed translations, then run `npm run check`. When publishing changed shards, advance the source-note-specific cache version in `dist/app.mjs` and the app script version in `dist/index.html`. The builder also accepts a complete English-to-Chinese translation dictionary; it refuses preview placeholders in a publication build. The committed shards are the durable translation data, and frozen historical inputs are not rewritten.
