# V17 early China and Africa / West–Central Asia / Europe research

## Scope and method

This file records the release-11 / v17 review of 34 previously thin directory profiles (baseline thinProfiles indices 0–33), followed by 18 world stories and 10 connected readings. The biographies preserve every stable ID and bilingual name. They replace directory scaffolding with original bilingual prose; no donor biography text, itinerary, geometry or code is copied. Sources below were opened and the relevant passage inspected. The records use the established 2026-11-09 review-cycle date; work continued into 10 November UTC.

The biography sources are transmitted ancient histories, specialist reference works and museum catalogues. A transmitted speech is evidence of the historian’s narrative, not an audio transcript. Regnal years are converted to conventional civil years; inherited directory lifespans were not used as evidence. Map points are representative settings, and no journey is inferred from a biography.

Three dated milestones are supplied where the evidence supports them. Five declared exceptions preserve source limits instead of inventing dates. This is a completion of the assigned early-profile backlog, not a claim that any person’s entire life is now exhaustively documented.

## Decisions that change interpretation

- Mencius uses the dated court-encounter interval, not a precise traditional lifespan. Qu Yuan’s 278 BCE endpoint is explicitly traditional and approximate. Sun Bin’s two battles remain conventional dates with conflicting narrative detail.
- Shang Yang’s text is not treated as a verbatim personal manifesto; the Book of Lord Shang developed across generations. Qin royal accession (247 BCE) is distinguished from first regnal year (246 BCE).
- Wang Jian is kept distinct from Wang Ben; Li Mu’s and the Chu campaigns’ divergent historical arrangements are noted. Ancient army and casualty totals are not represented as measured statistics.
- Zhang Qian’s first mission remains approximate because 139 and 138 BCE appear in modern chronologies. A failed alliance mission did not single-handedly create a transcontinental “Silk Road.”
- Sima Qian uses a career interval, without unsupported birth/death or a single book-completion year. Wang Mang’s restrictions on sale of people are not called abolition of slavery.
- Three Kingdoms biographies use Chen Shou and Pei Songzhi, including the annotations’ own source criticism. Guan Yu’s unnamed physician is not renamed Hua Tuo; fictional oaths and stratagems do not supply dates.
- Sun Quan’s Wuchang means the Ezhou region. Lu Xun is the third-century general 陆逊, not the modern writer 鲁迅. Sima Yi was a Wei minister in life; the imperial title in Jinshu is retrospective.
- Wang Xizhi’s 353 preface is separated from surviving later copies; the alleged burial of the autograph is not a fact claim. Tao Yuanming’s literary rural scenes are not turned into daily itinerary entries.
- Xie An coordinated government during the Fei River campaign; Xie Shi, Xie Xuan and others commanded at the front. Liu Yu’s Liu-Song is distinct from the dynasty founded in 960.

## Profile claim-to-source map

### Mencius / 孟子 — `china-person-mengzi`

Focus: ruler encounters datable to 323–314 BCE in the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy. The single approximate marker stands for that interval; traditional birth and death dates are not treated as established.

Milestones: -320 (approximate) — Counsel at Warring States courts.

- [Mencius](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mencius/) — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Sections on life, moral psychology and political philosophy support the dialogues, cultivation and humane government; the work has a layered transmission.
- [Mencius](https://iep.utm.edu/mencius/) — Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Biographical section dates ruler encounters to 323–314 BCE and distinguishes them from uncertain traditional lifespan dates.

### Shang Yang / 商鞅 — `china-person-shangyang`

Focus: the conventional reform period, 356–338 BCE; 350 BCE marks the Xianyang and county-administration measures. Regnal chronology is read with Shiji chapters 5 and 68. No inherited birth year is retained.

Milestones: -356 — Reform and rewards; -350 — A capital and county officials; -338 — Loss of royal protection.

- [Sima Qian, Shiji, chapter 68](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷068) — Transmitted ancient text; Wikisource edition. Reform provisions, the Xianyang move, the Wei campaign and death after Duke Xiao; the biography also contains moralizing anecdotes, which are not treated as literal reports.
- [Sima Qian, Shiji, chapter 5](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷005) — Transmitted ancient text; Wikisource edition. Qin annals supply Duke Xiao’s sequence of reforms, Xianyang administration and death; civil-year labels follow conventional regnal conversion.
- [The Book of Lord Shang](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/the-book-of-lord-shang/9780231542333/) — Columbia University Press. Publisher summary of Yuri Pines’s edition dates compilation to the fourth–third centuries BCE and attributes the work to Shang Yang and followers.

### Sun Bin / 孙膑 — `china-person-sunbin`

Focus: Guiling and Maling, conventionally 353 and 341 BCE. These dates do not establish a lifespan. The battlefield markers represent regional settings, and the accounts differ in campaign details.

Milestones: -353 (approximate) — Guiling; -341 (approximate) — Maling.

- [Sima Qian, Shiji, chapter 65](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷065) — Transmitted ancient text; Wikisource edition. Sun Bin’s service with Tian Ji and the two battle narratives; reported speeches, the racing anecdote and the precise ambush details are literary testimony.
- [Signs, clues and traces: anticipation in ancient Chinese political and military texts](https://www.academia.edu/13490949/Signs_clues_and_traces_anticipation_in_ancient_Chinese_political_and_military_texts) — Albert Galvany / Early China, author-hosted article. Albert Galvany’s Early China study gives 353 and 341 BCE and compares Shiji, Zhanguo ce and Yinqueshan versions.
- [Sun Bin Art of War](https://en.chinaculture.org/created/2005-07/21/content_70807.htm) — China Culture. Documents the 1972 Yinqueshan recovery, incomplete slips and uncertainty over the second section’s attribution.

### Qu Yuan / 屈原 — `china-person-quyuan`

Focus: the early third century BCE and the traditional death date of 278 BCE. The exact lifespan and attribution of individual poems remain uncertain; 278 is marked approximate, not independently established by Shiji chapter 84.

Milestones: -278 (approximate) — A traditional endpoint.

- [Sima Qian, Shiji, chapter 84](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷084) — Transmitted ancient text; Wikisource edition. Retrospective account of Chu service, political displacement, Li Sao and later remembrance by Jia Yi. It does not establish an exact lifespan.
- [Qu Yuan](https://en.chinaculture.org/focus/focus/cities/2011-06/07/content_416231.htm) — China Culture. Provides the conventional 278 BCE death date and later festival association; its modern patriotic and Romanticist characterizations are not adopted as ancient identities.

### Wang Jian / 王翦 — `china-person-wangjian`

### Qin Shi Huang / 秦始皇 — `china-person-qinshihuang`

Focus: accession as Qin king in 247 BCE through death in 210 BCE, rather than a full lifespan. Regnal year 1 began in 246 BCE; accession year and first regnal year are distinct.

Milestones: -247 — King of Qin; -221 — First Emperor; -213 — Restrictions on books; -210 (approximate) — Death during a tour.

- [Sima Qian, Shiji, chapter 6](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷006) — Transmitted ancient text; Wikisource edition. - [An Introduction to the Qin Empire (221–206 BCE)](https://education.asianart.org/resources/an-introduction-to-the-qin-empire/) — Asian Art Museum. Museum account confirms accession in 247 BCE, final conquest in 221 BCE and the Xianyang administrative setting.
