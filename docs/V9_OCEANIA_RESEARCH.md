# V9 Oceania research and editorial register

Research date: 23 October 2026. File: `dist/data/expansion-oceania-v9.json`.

## Delivered scope

The batch contains **35 original bilingual entries**: 5 culture, 12 event, 2 idea, 10 person, 1 place, 5 polity. It cites **74 distinct URLs**. Every English narrative has at least 120 words; Chinese narratives synthesize the same claims. These counts measure authored content, not complete independent historical verification.

OC1 adds Pohnpei/Saudeleur and Tongan political context, Yap–Palau negotiated exchange, Carolinian knowledge transmission and a navigator biography. OC2 adds regional Indigenous engineering, archaeology, food/material knowledge, northern Australian–Asian trade, Massim exchange and New Ireland ceremonial art. OC3 adds plantation labour, wartime labour, land and sovereignty campaigns, constitutional relationships, regional cooperation and literary/political biographies.

Only five entries are political overviews. Three are major timeline candidates (Tuʻi Tonga, Australia and New Zealand); two are secondary political phases (Saudeleur monumental period and the FSM constitutional transition). The remaining events, people, places and cultural histories should stay outside the principal polity timeline. No polygon geometry, route legs, shared application files or existing source data were changed by this batch.

## Evidence and language limits

- Sources were searched through the authoritative web search and opened where retrievable. Retrieval status is recorded per citation: `page-text` means relevant extracted page text was inspected, not a claim to have read every linked document; `indexed-excerpt` means search-indexed text or metadata was inspected and the full source was not read. A successful URL lookup is not a verification of every historical claim.
- Of 74 distinct cited URLs, 43 supplied inspected page text and 31 supplied indexed excerpts/metadata. Some ANU biographies, SPC pages, Nature material and museum pages failed full-text extraction. Their evidence notes preserve that limit; no blocked page is described as fully read.
- Independent institutional corroboration varies. Some entries combine two pages from the same institution, a substantive source with a collection record, or a primary recollection with later scholarship. Those are different evidentiary roles, not two independent confirmations of every sentence. A larger bibliography does not erase a narrow source base.
- The Sista autobiographical extract, Polynesian Voyaging Society history, FSM testimony and Liliʻuokalani Trust account are situated participant/institutional perspectives. The data identify them as such. The retrospective FSM claim about trusteeship termination is not reused as an unqualified UN legal date.
- Museum records provide dates for material evidence; publication dates and institutional milestones do not establish a culture’s start or end. Oral traditions are described as traditions, not recast as radiometric dating.
- English and Chinese are original companion syntheses, not copied source translations. No poems, lyrics, historical images, restricted cultural knowledge or full source passages were reproduced. Transliterated names retain identifiable Latin-script names in the parallel English fields.

## Chronology and geography decisions

- Madjedbebe’s published c.65,000-year occupation estimate is outside the atlas’s 13,000 BCE lower limit. Its entry is dated to the 2017–2022 research window and states the distinction. It does not clamp occupation to an invented civil start year. The archaeological authors’ estimate is not represented as an uncontested date for all arrival in Australia.
- Brewarrina is dated to its 2005 heritage listing because a precise construction date was not established. The article explains that this is not the fishery’s beginning.
- Saudeleur 1200–1500 is a monumental phase, not an asserted complete dynasty lifetime. Tuʻi Tonga c.1200–1800 is a qualified political focus; archaeological interaction is not equated with annexation.
- Australia 1901–2026 and New Zealand 1947–2026 provide continuing political intervals. Their starts mean federation and a declared constitutional phase respectively; neither dates Indigenous history or authorizes current borders for the entire interval. Canberra is explicitly a current representative reference, not the federal capital throughout the span.
- All coordinates are representative. Personal milestones distinguish documented arrival/conference locations from home, cultural or institutional reference points; no continuous personal itinerary is inferred. Inter-island networks are narratives, not newly invented direct sailing lines.
- The 1967 Australian referendum is not described as granting citizenship or the vote. Liliʻuokalani’s 1893 overthrow is distinguished from her 1895 imprisonment. French testing dates end in January 1996; later monitoring is not projected into the testing interval.

## Remaining regional depth

This batch develops all three Oceania workstreams at bounded release scope. It does not exhaust Oceania. Future work should deepen New Guinea highland agriculture beyond existing Kuk, Torres Strait and Aboriginal regional histories, Micronesian and Polynesian local chronological sequences, women’s work beyond the selected biographies, and island-specific twentieth-century nation building. Source-language and community review remain priorities. Dated territorial reconstruction is a separate research task.
