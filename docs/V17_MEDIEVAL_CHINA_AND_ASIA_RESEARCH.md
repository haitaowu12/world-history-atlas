# V17 medieval China and Asia: source and chronology ledger

## Checkpoint scope

The biography checkpoint upgrades the existing 34 directory IDs from Northern Wei through late Ming; it adds no duplicate person IDs. Each has original bilingual prose, at least three inspected dated anchors, bilingual place labels and source-specific evidence notes. The selected intervals are often career or reign spans rather than asserted lifespans. All 34 passed JSON parsing, English word-count checks (80–150 words), milestone-count checks and reference validation against the recovered baseline directory. There are 118 milestones and no limited-dated-evidence exceptions in this batch.

The edition uses the agreed review-batch label `2026-11-09`. Retrieval records describe the actual method: opened institutional/scholarly pages, opened PDF passages, or downloaded page text. Search results were discovery aids; inaccessible pages and search snippets were not treated as completed source inspection. Modern translations and museum catalogue prose were consulted as evidence and were not reproduced. The newly written atlas prose is governed by the atlas data policy; the linked sources and images retain their own rights.

## Editorial decisions that change interpretation

- Distinguish original work, surviving copy and later memorial: Wang Wei’s Wangchuan poems are not the date of a surviving seventeenth-century painting; Yan Zhenqing’s 758 draft remembers deaths in 756; Su Shi’s Red Cliff essay was composed in 1082 and the inspected manuscript was written in 1083.
- Use supported activity spans where birth or death conventions conflict: Wu Zetian, Wang Wei, Su Shi, Genghis Khan and Qi Jiguang. No contested lifespan was retained merely because it existed in the directory.
- Keep Guo Ziyi’s 755 anchor explicitly tied to the outbreak of the An Lushan rebellion, not an unsupported appointment date. His birth and death are supported by the Palace Museum entry.
- Keep Liu Zongyuan’s Yongzhou exile distinct from his later Liuzhou governorship. Do not turn Liu Yuxi’s participation in reform politics into an unsupported dated personal milestone.
- Wang Anshi’s crop-loan memorial supplies an intended mechanism, not proof that its benefits were universally achieved. The general page’s erroneous 1068 chief-councillor introduction is not used.
- Sima Guang’s 1070 anchor concerns his request to leave capital politics; it is not a claim that the chronicle began that year. The directory entry’s incorrect 405 BCE starting date for the chronicle was omitted.
- The Song founding banquet is treated through Charles Hartman’s analysis of its later historical construction. Yue Fei’s reputation is distinguished from later national and tattoo narratives.
- Lu You’s dated 1172 and 1202 poems were checked in Stephen Owen’s anthology, including the actual poem headings and accompanying notes; the modern English translations were not copied.
- Wen Tianxiang’s death uses the modern civil-calendar year 1283, with the conventional 1282 lunar-year discrepancy explained. Qi Jiguang’s source gives conflicting 1587/1588 death dates, so the profile closes at the inspected 1583 posting instead.
- Yongle’s accession in 1402 differs from the first year of his era in 1403. Palace completion in 1420 differs from the formal capital move in 1421.
- Zhang Juzheng’s 1581 Single Whip measure is an extension of fiscal consolidation, not the invention or instantaneous uniform adoption of silver taxation.
- Some consulted summaries contain errors outside the retained claims: the Taizong handout’s 624 succession date, the Suzhou biography’s conflation of Fan Zhongyan’s 1034 school with a charitable estate, the Yue Fei page’s emperor/date for rehabilitation, and the Xin Qiji genealogy article’s proposed Guangde appointment. These were not imported.
