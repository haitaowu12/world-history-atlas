# China map gaps: dated, reusable references and alignment

Reviewed 28 October 2026. This review adds source-dated illustrations, not an annual reconstruction or a claim that old atlases settle disputed frontiers. The still-unfilled periods below remain visible as gaps. No retired Jiangshan geometry was restored.

## What was checked upstream

Jiangshan main was checked at `58cc9a09634d063118f088f8b24e117153cd5dba`, one commit after the previously reviewed `9799f0f61cff80922970d2a0b304e18527db7e1e`. ## Song, Jin and Western Xia — 1141 reference

The new `china-open-song-jin-1141-v18.geojson` contains three outlines, each duplicated only to express two non-contiguous reviewed display windows. It extracts the **original 2007 version** of [Ian Kiu's 1141 map](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jinn_and_Sung_Dynasties_1141.png), not the later replacement version. The file page attributes the work to Kiu, identifies its basis as Herrmann's 1935 atlas, and offers CC BY 3.0. Both the pinned illustration and Kiu's separately released [blank base](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blank_China_with_terrain_and_bcoast.png) are retained in `references/china-song-1141/`. Their SHA-256 hashes are, respectively, `e8530c4c9203ae3f9c779db718782b2df647b5e106f34a2ca934560455aa4eb4` and `4a133e874f18c9b5f5c1d4dbc4f7054d72e388a35ea63780f6866bed3988dddb`. Attribution, a direct license link, and a change description are provided in the data notice and feature detail.

- Reference periods: 1141–1160 and 1165–1205; Western Xia's second period ends in 1204. These are editorial windows for a **fixed 1141 map**, not an assertion that each border stayed fixed. The 1161–1164 conflict is left unpainted by this particular source. The [2026 peer-reviewed archaeological study of Jin frontier management](https://brill.com/view/journals/tpao/112/1-2/article-p163_6.xml) describes the 1165 Song–Jin treaty, the subsequent coexistence and the uncertain, non-linear character of Jin's northern border. Cambridge's [Song chronology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/middle-imperial-china-9001350/song-in-a-multipolar-world/734109916CAD14A419B6FD1C4F720946) confirms the wider political sequence. The 1205 Mongol raids on Xia make 1204 a conservative endpoint; the source illustration itself does not establish the year-by-year frontier.
- Source limits: the small source graphic cuts off northeastern Jin at the right edge; its straight source-crop edge is not a surveyed border. Song/Jin/Xia lines and influence were complex and changed. This 1935-derived interpretation should be replaced when more detailed, independently licensed scholarly GIS becomes available.
- Registration: a spherical Lambert azimuthal model with independently fitted rotation and scale was fitted to modern coastline and political lines on Kiu's blank base. Its parameters are in `scripts/build-song-jin-1141.py`. The two island silhouettes, southeastern China coast, and selected inland reference lines were fitting evidence. **Withheld** Natural Earth coastline samples averaged 1.16 source pixels on the north China coast, 1.13 on Korea, and 1.22 on Vietnam; sample resolution and coast generalization limit the meaning of those figures. Inland fit was separately inspected against Mongolia, Nepal and Southeast Asia: it is better than a coast-only affine fit, which placed inland borders many pixels away. Approximate capital checks place Hangzhou and Chengdu in Song, Beijing and Kaifeng in Jin, and Yinchuan in Xia. The method does not verify historical boundary vertices, and the source has only 556 × 537 pixels.
- Extraction: source color masks and external contours remove label holes; a 3-pixel close handles raster interruptions. No modern administrative boundary supplies a historical border. The exact source and output hashes are checked by the builder and manifest.

## Ming — 1415 reference, including occupied Đại Việt

`china-open-ming-1415-v18.geojson` extracts the main yellow area and separate Hainan island from [Herrmann and Georg Westermann, *Historical and Commercial Atlas of China* (1935), map 38](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_Ming_Chinese_empire_1415.jpg). Wikimedia Commons identifies the 1935 source as public domain in the US on a non-renewal basis and applies a life-plus-70-years tag; other jurisdictions can differ. Its renewal classification has not been independently confirmed against the registry, which was inaccessible. The source file is retained with SHA-256 `b37627340c49d28ffba1a45b7c75e87e0dda408a6b5e0ead59c7e82faadb3180` and its notice records those limits. Do not treat the US tag as a universal legal guarantee.

The dated reference is displayed for **1415–1417**. [Columbia University's Vietnam timeline](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/timelines/vietnam_timeline.htm) places Ming occupation in 1407–1427 and the Lam Sơn resistance from 1418. The fixed 1415 depiction is stopped before that resistance rather than purporting to show later military control. The old map combines frontier claims or garrisons with directly administered core areas; the far northeast is interrupted by the printed inset. The adaptation does not include the distant yellow areas in Sumatra, nor does it claim equal control everywhere.

Registration used printed marks for Beijing, Kaifeng, Xi'an and Nanjing, plus Hainan and Taiwan island centers. An empirical Lambert azimuthal fit gave 2.6–6.7 pixels of point residual on the 1,577 × 2,339 scan; those are registration checks, not estimates of historical border accuracy. A withheld Natural Earth coastline overlay was inspected across China, Korea, Vietnam and adjacent seas. The yellow region was extracted by color, closed over small text breaks, simplified, and kept as the source's mainland and Hainan; the inset and unrelated distant areas were excluded. The fitted model, color thresholds and checksums are reproducible in `scripts/build-ming-1415.py`. Per-pixel conversion or closing can generalize small coastal features and must not be mistaken for a cadastral survey.

## Gaps and candidates not adopted

- **400 BCE:** no source was found that both supplies credible date-specific Chinese state boundaries and has a clear reusable geometry lineage. One CC0 world map explicitly shows Wu as an independent state at 400 BCE, long after Yue's conquest; another widely used animation has no cited basis for holding its 451 BCE frame through 400 BCE. A separately licensed 350 BCE illustration has largely open border strokes, not complete state polygons, and is not a 400 BCE map. GlobalSecurity labels an image as 400 BCE, but it is visually the same 1935 atlas plate that is explicitly titled 350 BCE; that label is not reliable dating evidence. Do not relabel, close, or interpolate these into unsupported borders.
- **Later Ming, including 1600:** a 1580 illustration is labeled CC BY-SA, but its declared source chain passes through a 1580 map with no identified creator or machine-readable source. It was not used to fill 1600. A 1415 map that includes northern Vietnam must not be extrapolated two centuries.
- The 350 BCE plate in the same 1935 atlas has a life-plus-70 tag but lacks an explicit US public-domain tag on its Commons file page. It was not imported; its conflation of the state of Yue with southern Baiyue also needs scholarly review.
- An ostensibly open 1200 map labels Nanzhao, which no longer existed by then, and another Song derivative traces back to the modern Tan Qixiang atlas. They were not used as substitutes.

These are coverage and provenance gaps, not claims that no people or political communities existed.

## Delivery and runtime considerations

The two derived geometry downloads add 190,090 bytes uncompressed in total to initial map data; the old Song raster and Ming scan are retained outside `dist` for reproducibility. Existing generic map layers and the normal neighbor-coloring mechanism are reused. A detail-panel review also found that substring matching treated “ming” inside “farming” as a Ming story; matching whole identity tokens now avoids those false associations while preserving explicit linked stories.
