## hagia-sophia-justinian ← atlantic-rebuild-met-464495
- Role: named-person
- Evidence: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/hagia-sophia-532-37
- Explanation: The ivory names Justinian, the patron who ordered Hagia Sophia rebuilt. Its 521 consular display offers an earlier example of his public titles, before the church’s 532–537 rebuilding. It is neither architectural fabric nor a view of the church.
- 中文: 象牙板铭记下令重建圣索菲亚教堂的赞助者查士丁尼；521年执政官身份展示，早于532至537年教堂重建。它既非教堂建筑构件，也非教堂景观图。

## seneca-falls-convention ← frederick-douglass

- Evidence: https://www.nps.gov/wori/learn/historyculture/report-of-the-womans-rights-convention.htm

## seneca-falls-convention ← atlantic-rebuild-declaration-of-independence
- Role: related-work
- Evidence: https://www.nps.gov/articles/declaration-of-sentiments.htm
- Explanation: The 1776 Declaration supplied a rhetorical model for the 1848 Declaration of Sentiments. Viewing the earlier document invites comparison of whose equality each declaration claimed. This parchment is the Declaration of Independence, not the convention’s own statement.
- 中文: 1776年《独立宣言》为1848年《感伤宣言》提供修辞范本。观看较早文献，可比较两份宣言分别主张谁的平等。这张羊皮纸是《独立宣言》，而非大会自身声明。

## Review qualifications

- The two Justinian associations use a named, inscribed 521 consular object, not a portrait, legal manuscript or architectural photograph. The law entry explicitly discusses inherited official traditions; the church entry identifies the same commissioner. The contexts retain the temporal gap. Source corroboration for the object and its titles: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/464495 and https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-byzantine-state-under-justinian-i-justinian-the-great .
- The Persepolis ration association is an explicit comparison already made in the entry, not a claim that a palace relief documents rations. Tablet dates checked against https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/persepolis-elamite-tablets/ ; the relief is roughly a century later.
- Seneca Falls gains two useful images but counts as one newly covered entry. Douglass’s photograph is c.1879, thirty years after the convention.
- Liliʻuokalani’s sitting, later inscription and overthrow dates are distinguished.
- Camp Saxton links the proclamation document, not the actual camp reading copy, and makes no first-reading claim.
