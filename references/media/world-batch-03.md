# World batch 03: historic places

Prepared 2026-11-11. Nineteen unchanged, authentic site photographs for nineteen previously uncovered exact atlas entries. All are permissively licensed CC BY-SA photographs, not generated illustrations.

## Integration boundary

Only files named by visual-assets-world-batch-03-v18.json are admitted. Copy those exact assets into dist/assets/history-media and the shard into dist/data during owner integration. The research assets folder also contains the excluded Taputapuātea candidate: do not glob-copy it. No code, dist, Git, build workflow or deployment was changed by this worker.

## Evidence and checks

Assembled entry snapshots: entry-evidence.json, loaded using scripts/lib/atlas-records.mjs. Original Commons image metadata: individual *-metadata.json files; exact page revisions and license templates retained in commons-source-pages.json; selected.json binds the final choices. UNESCO *-context.html pages preserve the exact-site historical evidence. entry-decisions.json records each admission and rejected candidates. Final pixels were reviewed on contact-sheet-final.jpg; no local crop, resize, recolor or synthetic content is delivered. Commons thumbnails sometimes encode 960 pixels despite an 800-pixel request; recorded dimensions come from decoding the actual bytes, not the API hint.

Photo copyrights are separate from ancient architectural subjects. All intended architectural subjects are ancient or medieval, identified in the UNESCO descriptions; photo author, date and selected license are preserved individually. Source edits are credited, including Satdeep Gill’s cropped Angkor view and the Rainer Halama/UnpetitproleX Thanjavur derivative. Borobudur metadata gives an original upload date, not a proven capture date, and is labeled accordingly.

Review establishes image identity and local entry relevance, not wholesale verification of donor records or historical certainty for all underlying claims.

## Useful knowledge gaps for future targeted work

- Dholavira and Mohenjo-daro: existing entries explain water systems but do not yet walk readers through a reservoir/bath cross-section. A sourced diagram could distinguish storage, access and drainage without guessing at institutions.
- Nalanda: a named teacher, curriculum or manuscript case study would connect surviving temple bricks to the actual work of learning. This image alone does not establish classroom use for Temple 12.
- Great Zimbabwe and Kilwa: pairing the sites with securely provenanced excavated imports would make the Indian Ocean trade claim materially visible. Do not attach generic imported porcelain by region alone.
- Caral: the selected photograph makes excavation and conservation visible, but a stronger whole-city plan or stepped-platform overview remains desirable.
- Taputapuātea remains uncovered by this batch. A securely identified, properly licensed whole-marae view on Raʻiātea is preferable to the close-up object whose date is unclear.
