## Scope and blockers

The 35–50 image research target was not reached. Further institutional image downloads encountered 403 access restrictions. Those requests were stopped and no access-control bypass is proposed. Images already downloaded were retained and audited. Cleveland accession 1996.350 uses the institution’s displayed 640-pixel derivative; its direct CDN URL had failed earlier. This successful public-page derivative is documented rather than concealed. No additional alternate retrieval was attempted after the consolidated restriction was identified.

All admitted originals remain byte-for-byte as downloaded. Every file is below 750,000 bytes and 3,000 pixels on its longest side. Contact-sheet thumbnails are review aids, not replacements for original files. No generated historical image, fake portrait or synthesized restoration is included.

## Interpretive flags

- The 1436 Timur manuscript depicts a 1403 episode retrospectively. Bijapur is a symbolic dynastic assembly, not a contemporaneous meeting.
- The Theban relief was damaged and restored in antiquity. The Ahmose fragment comes from his funerary temple, not a battle scene.
- The Pekalongan cloth is an earlier unsigned comparison, not the narrative’s signed 1910 garment. The Bengal maker and family are unrecorded.
- Palmyra origin remains “probably.” Ban Chiang culture attribution is not a securely documented excavated findspot. The Mexica model is not an identified Tenochtitlan temple.
- The Seljuq source title calls the gold coin a dinar but its prose calls it a dirham; use “gold coin,” as in the shard, rather than reproduce the inconsistent denomination.
- Wari standardization suggests organized production but does not prove this garment came from a particular state workshop.
- Held: Nok date conflict, uncertain Djenné dating/origin, late Jain cover, late portable Buddhist shrine, Hattusha association too indirect, and image-specific restricted/non-open sources.
