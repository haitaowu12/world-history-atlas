# World batch 14: Goryeo painting and Ayyubid ceramic work

Reviewed 11 November 2026. Three CC0 images illuminate two previously uncovered entries. Full local evidence: `research/v18/world-batch-14/source-rights-ledger.json`.

## Amita and eight bodhisattvas

- Target: `goryeo-kingdom`.
- Object: [F1906.269, National Museum of Asian Art](https://asia.si.edu/object/F1906.269/), Korea, mid–late fourteenth century; ink, colour and gold on silk. Artist, original temple and patron not identified.
- The museum’s label identifies Amita, eight bodhisattvas and Kshitigarbha’s simple hooded robe. The painting directly supports the entry’s Buddhist-arts narrative.
- The object and its selected image explicitly carry CC0 in the [official item-bearing bulk record](https://smithsonian-open-access.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/metadata/edan/fsg/18.txt). Gift of Charles Lang Freer.
- [Further reading: Korea, 1000–1400](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/07/eak.html).

## Ceramic craft in Ayyubid Syria

- Target: `ayyubid-dynasty`.
- [F1931.7, a many-spouted lamp](https://asia.si.edu/object/F1931.7/): Syria, twelfth–thirteenth century; underglaze-painted stone-paste. The maker, workshop, original building and royal owner are not named. Purchase, Charles Lang Freer Endowment. [Object/image CC0 record](https://smithsonian-open-access.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/metadata/edan/fsg/04.txt).
- [F1909.376, a pitcher catalogued as a waster](https://asia.si.edu/object/F1909.376/): Raqqa, Syria, twelfth–thirteenth century; stone-paste painted black under blue glaze. Its collapsed form makes production failure visible. Raqqa is the catalogue origin, not a documented excavation findspot. Gift of Charles Lang Freer. [Object/image CC0 record](https://smithsonian-open-access.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/metadata/edan/fsg/08.txt).
- [Further reading: Ayyubid art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-ayyubid-period-ca-1171-1260).
