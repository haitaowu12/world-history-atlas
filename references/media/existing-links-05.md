The production catalogue advanced during research from checkpoint 16 to the World09-inclusive baseline: **308 assets / 667 of 2,087 entries**. This shard alone reaches **672 of 2,087**. ## Proposed connections

- Maurya: empire and public inscriptions → Lumbini’s Ashokan pillar. A surviving royal inscription and Buddhist commemorative monument directly illustrates the story’s principal evidence.
- Ashoka biography → the same pillar, explicitly distinguished from the biography’s Kalinga edict.
- Early Buddhism: teachings and communities → the same third-century BCE pillar. The story explicitly compares early traditions with later royal inscriptions and pilgrimage evidence; the image does not claim to depict the Buddha’s life.
- New Zealand women’s parliamentary vote → Kate Sheppard’s 1893 franchise report cover. This is a document of the named campaign, not a generic portrait or the Electoral Act itself.
- Buddhist art: stupas and images in Gandhara and Mathura → Sanchi’s actual gateway, railing and Great Stupa. The story’s opening comparison concerns these architectural forms before turning to figurative images. Caption explicitly identifies Sanchi in central India, not Gandhara or Mathura. This is the most contextual of the five proposals; the owner can omit it if requiring an exact named site in prose rather than a direct architectural comparison.

## Deliberate exclusions

Prior passes’ restrictions on unrelated works by the same author, same-place substitutions and generic subject imagery remain in force. In particular, Aksum stelae do not illustrate Ezana’s Christian coins; Timbuktu’s mosque does not show the specific legal manuscripts. The rejected/held ledger also excludes later Gupta and Gandharan sculptures from the early art window, a Nimrud guardian from the Nineveh library, a medieval Shiva from contemporary Boston Hindu communities, the Qingming bridge scene from an examinations/officials story, and unsupported identification of pictured Qorikancha masonry with Pachacuti’s rebuilding. The broad Mali and Songhai records do not gain a mosque merely from regional overlap.

UNESCO Lumbini’s precise Buddha birth date is not adopted: the atlas correctly keeps the relevant chronology qualified, and the association depends only on the Ashokan pillar and pilgrimage. No correction to target prose is needed for the five proposals.
