## Editorial choices and follow-up

- Lanting is a Tang copy attributed to Feng Chengsu, not an autograph. The Hong Kong Palace Museum narrative source in the biography discusses a different copy, attributed to Yu Shinan; these were not conflated.
- Tao Yuanming is a 1650 literary painting; Ban Zhao is a 1799 teaching scene. Neither depicts a witnessed ancient event.
- Xu Guangqi is dated only to the seventeenth century in the source. Historical-artwork is intentional: lifetime versus posthumous status remains unknown.
- Du Fu’s Yuan attribution was checked against the Palace Museum record. Mencius’s post-1330 album dating was checked against the NPM exhibition linked from Commons.
- Bai Juyi’s poem is written by Wen Zhengming, not Bai himself. Photograph credit is Editor at Large, CC BY-SA 2.5. Permanent collection is not identified in the photo source.
- Guo Shoujing and Li Qingzhao are modern memorial photographs. Photo date and sculpture date are distinguished; Li’s sculptor and installation year remain unknown. The source photo licenses are CC BY 2.5 and CC0 respectively.
- The GFDL-only Cui Hui reproduction and poorly dated Sima Qian portrait were rejected.
- Exact named-person links are not licenses to accept every inherited claim. The evidence receipt separately flags the composite Qu Yuan chronology, Mencius’s asserted Jixia role and compilation, and Li Qingzhao’s precise Wujiang poem itinerary. No broad dynasty or era association was admitted.
- No titled atlas entry was found for Cao Zhenxiu, Gai Qi, Wen Zhengming or Chen Hongshou. These are concrete art-production and historical-memory gaps suitable for later sourced additions.
