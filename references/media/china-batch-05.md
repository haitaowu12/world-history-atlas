## Content flags

These are separate editorial review items, not newly verified chronology changes. They were not used to manufacture extra image associations.
- `china-landmark-197`: Chinese title includes a crane in the Six-One sobriquet inventory. Ouyang’s well-known Six-One account enumerates books, collected inscriptions, qin, chess, wine, and the old man himself, not a crane. The admitted 1072 letter verifies retirement context, not this inventory. Check Ouyang Xiu’s Liuyi jushi zhuan before editing.
- `china-landmark-127`: English calls Han Yu’s Left at Lantian Pass for My Grandnephew Xiang a quatrain; the quoted poem is an eight-line regulated poem. Check the full text of 左遷至藍關示姪孫湘.
- `china-landmark-427`: English and Chinese titles appear to foreground different Zeng Guofan events: ordnance activity versus siege context. Reconcile the exact event and dating with the record’s cited source.
- `china-landmark-292`: Xiao He’s moonlight pursuit of Han Xin is strongly shaped by later drama. Distinguish the early recruitment account from the later dramatic scene. Compare Shiji / Hanshu with the later drama tradition.
- `china-landmark-293`: The familiar secret Chencang stratagem should not be treated as an unqualified documented action by Han Xin without primary-source review. Check which parts occur in early sources versus later storytelling.
- `china-event-zhanguo-007`: The literal claim that Sun Bin shot Pang Juan dead requires correction or qualification against the early account of Pang Juan’s death at Maling. Read Shiji, biographies of Sun Wu and Wu Qi / Sun Bin; distinguish battlefield death narrative from a direct personal shooting.
- `china-event-zhanguo-006`: Avoid silently collapsing the persons, tactics and outcomes of Guiling and Maling. The Sun Bin biography already distinguishes later military stories from excavated texts. Cross-check the event against that reviewed biography and primary accounts.
